# Fact sheet: Expunged, Sealed, Pardoned (and related terms)

Research date: 2026-11-20. Every fact below was read on the page cited. Quotes are verbatim from the fetched page text. Items marked **[UNCERTAIN]** need a re-check before publishing.

---

## 1. Definitions (official sources; terms differ by state)

### Pardon (federal)
- "A pardon is an expression of the President's forgiveness and can be granted in recognition of the applicant's acceptance of responsibility for the crime and established good conduct for a significant period of time after conviction or completion of sentence." Source: https://www.justice.gov/pardon/frequently-asked-questions
- Constitution, Art. II, sec. 2: the President "shall have Power to grant Reprieves and Pardons for Offences against the United States, except in Cases of Impeachment." Source: https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript
- DOJ Office of Legal Counsel (Aug. 11, 2006): a pardon "removes, either conditionally or unconditionally, the punitive legal consequences that would otherwise flow from conviction," but "does not erase the conviction as a historical fact." Source: https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/olc/opinions/2006/08/31/pardonopfinal_0.pdf

### Commutation (federal)
- "A commutation of sentence reduces a sentence, either totally or partially, that is then being served, but it does not change the fact of conviction, imply innocence, or remove civil disabilities that apply to the convicted person as a result of the criminal conviction." Source: https://www.justice.gov/pardon/frequently-asked-questions

### Pardon (state example, Georgia)
- "A pardon is an order of official forgiveness and may be granted to individuals who have maintained a good reputation in their community and have remained crime free for a required period of years following the completion of their sentence(s)." Georgia State Board of Pardons and Paroles, FY2025 Annual Report, p. 28. Source: https://pap.georgia.gov/document/document/pardons-paroles-ar-2025-d12-1pdf/download

### Expungement
- **Federal statute (18 U.S.C. 3607(c))**: the expungement order "shall direct that there be expunged from all official records, except the nonpublic records referred to in subsection (b), all references to his arrest for the offense, the institution of criminal proceedings against him, and the results thereof. The effect of the order shall be to restore such person, in the contemplation of the law, to the status he occupied before such arrest or institution of criminal proceedings." Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/3607
- **Ohio (statute)**: for sections 2953.33 to 2953.521, "expunge" includes "To destroy, delete, and erase a record as appropriate for the record's physical or electronic form or characteristic" so the record cannot be retrieved (wording via page summary; check exact text). Source: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2953.31
- **Ohio (court self-help)**: "When a record is expunged, it is destroyed forever." "No one will have access to an expunged record because it no longer exists." Franklin County Municipal Court self-help article. Source: https://stagemunicipalcourt.franklincountyohio.gov/Departments-Services/Self-Help/Self-Help-Articles/Sealing-vs-Expungement-What’s-the-difference **[UNCERTAIN: the URL is a "stage" subdomain; the production domain now redirects to franklincountymunicourt.org and the article was not found there. Cite with care or replace.]**
- **Illinois (legal aid site, not a court)**: "You might even be able to have these records destroyed. This is called expungement." Source: https://www.illinoislegalaid.org/legal-information/expunging-or-sealing-criminal-record
- **California**: "true expungement does not exist in California." California Courts self-help. Source: https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/clean-your-record
- **Arizona**: Arizona's set-aside statute says DPS "may not redact or remove any part of the person's record" (see Set-aside below). Source: https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00905.htm

### Sealing
- **Ohio (court self-help)**: with sealing, "the record is not destroyed. Instead, it is locked away so that most people can no longer access the record." "Most employers cannot see sealed records." Source: same Franklin County URL above **[UNCERTAIN: staging URL]**
- **Illinois (legal aid)**: "You can hide some of these charges from being seen by the general public. This is called sealing." Source: https://www.illinoislegalaid.org/legal-information/expunging-or-sealing-criminal-record
- **New York (CPL 160.59, Dept. of State flyer)**: "The criminal record of your prior crime or crimes cannot be seen by anyone other than qualified state agencies and state and local law enforcement, unless you are seeking to become a police or peace officer, applying for a gun permit, or the information is needed for law enforcement purposes." Eligibility: crime-free 10 years; at most two convictions, at most one felony; "Sex offenses, violent felonies and serious felonies are not eligible for sealing." Source: https://dos.ny.gov/criminal-records-sealed-after-10-years
- **Arizona (A.R.S. 13-911 sealing, court self-service center)**: a person "may petition the court to have their criminal case record sealed"; if granted, the petitioner may state on employment, housing and financial aid or loan applications that they have never been arrested for, charged with or convicted of the crime (summary of page). Source: https://www.azcourts.gov/selfservicecenter/Criminal-Law/Criminal-Law-Forms

### Nondisclosure (Texas term for sealing)
- "An order of nondisclosure is a court order prohibiting public entities, including courts, clerks of the court, law enforcement agencies, and prosecutorial offices, from disclosing certain criminal records." "An order of nondisclosure legally frees you from having to disclose certain information about your criminal history in response to questions on job applications." Texas Office of Court Administration, "An Overview of Orders of Nondisclosure" (revised August 2026), p. 1. Source: https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf

### Set-aside / dismissal / vacatur
- **Arizona set-aside**: a person "may ask the court to set aside the judgment of guilt and release the convicted person from all penalties and disabilities resulting from the conviction"; "court and criminal history records no longer indicate a conviction, but that the conviction was set aside." Source: https://www.azcourts.gov/selfservicecenter/Criminal-Law/Criminal-Law-Forms
- **Arizona statute**: "The department of public safety must update the person's criminal history with an annotation that the conviction has been set aside and, if applicable, a certificate of second chance has been issued but may not redact or remove any part of the person's record." Source: https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00905.htm
- **California dismissal (PC 1203.4, often called "expungement")**: the conviction is "withdrawn and set aside"; it does not "Remove the conviction from California or FBI criminal history records" and does not "Seal the court file from public inspection"; the dismissal "will continue to appear on your criminal history." Superior Court of Tulare County. Source: https://www.tulare.courts.ca.gov/petition-dismissal-under-pc-ss12034
- **Washington vacatur (RCW 9.94A.640(4)(a))**: after vacatur, "the fact that the offender has been convicted of the offense shall not be included in the offender's criminal history"; the offender "shall be released from all penalties and disabilities resulting from the offense"; and "may state that the offender has never been convicted of that crime." But "Nothing in this section affects or prevents the use of an offender's prior conviction in a later criminal prosecution." Source: https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9.94A.640

### Certificate of rehabilitation (California)
- "A Certificate of Rehabilitation is a court order that says someone who was (1) convicted of a felony ..." [served time] "... has been rehabilitated." It "does not erase your criminal record," does not "Erase a felony conviction or seal a criminal record," and does not restore gun rights. It is sent to the Governor and "is considered an official application for a pardon from the governor." Source: https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/clean-your-record/certificate-rehabilitation

## 2. What each does in practice

### Who can still see a sealed record
- **Texas**: after an order of nondisclosure, "DPS has 10 business days to seal, not delete, the criminal history record information"; "some agencies, including law enforcement agencies, will still have access to the information, although the information is sealed." The list of such agencies is at Texas Government Code 411.0765. Source: https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf (p. 24)
- **New York**: sealed convictions remain visible to "qualified state agencies and state and local law enforcement," and for police/peace officer applications, gun permits, or law enforcement purposes. Source: https://dos.ny.gov/criminal-records-sealed-after-10-years
- **Illinois (legal aid)**: "Any employer required by law to conduct fingerprint background checks can see sealed felony" records; law enforcement, courts and certain agencies also keep access; "federal agencies, like immigration authorities, may still have access to expunged or sealed records" because "state orders do not apply to federal databases." Source: https://www.illinoislegalaid.org/legal-information/after-your-expungement-or-sealing-case-decided-common-questions

### Is an expunged record destroyed?
- **Illinois (legal aid)**: "an expunged record is erased or destroyed by law enforcement agencies, and their records cannot be reopened." Courts do not destroy them but must impound them: "the records cannot be accessed without a court order." Source: https://www.illinoislegalaid.org/legal-information/after-your-expungement-or-sealing-case-decided-common-questions
- **Federal (18 U.S.C. 3607)**: references are expunged from official records except a DOJ "nonpublic record" kept to check later eligibility. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/3607
- **Ohio**: the statutory definition says destroy, delete and erase (see above). Source: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2953.31
- **Texas expunction**: NOT VERIFIED on a primary page in this session. A search summary of a 2014 OCA Senate testimony said agencies must destroy or return records. **[UNCERTAIN: verify Tex. Code Crim. Proc. ch. 55/55A before use]** https://www.txcourts.gov/media/662319/OCA-Written-Testimony-Expunctions-and-Non-Disclosure.pdf

### Does a pardon remove the record? Generally no.
- **Federal**: "Does a presidential pardon expunge or erase the conviction for which the pardon was granted? No." "If you were to be granted a presidential pardon, the pardoned offense would not be removed from your criminal record. Instead, both the federal conviction as well as the pardon would both appear on your record." Source: https://www.justice.gov/pardon/frequently-asked-questions
- **Federal (OLC 2006)**: "A presidential pardon granted under Article II, § 2 of the Constitution does not automatically expunge Judicial or Executive Branch records relating to the conviction or underlying offense." Source: https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/olc/opinions/2006/08/31/pardonopfinal_0.pdf
- **Georgia**: "A pardon does not expunge, remove, or erase crimes from a person's criminal record." Source: https://pap.georgia.gov/document/document/pardons-paroles-ar-2025-d12-1pdf/download (p. 28)
- **Exception, Pennsylvania**: "As of June 11, 2024, records related to offenses pardoned by the Governor will be expunged without further action by the applicant." (cites 18 Pa.C.S. 9122(a.1)). Source: https://www.bop.pa.gov/application-process/Pages/Frequently-Asked-Questions.aspx
- **Exception, Illinois**: the legal aid FAQ lists "expunged pardoned convictions" among records law enforcement can still see, which implies Illinois pardons can lead to expungement (from a search summary). **[UNCERTAIN: not read directly; verify on the source page]** https://www.illinoislegalaid.org/legal-information/after-your-expungement-or-sealing-case-decided-common-questions

## 3. Federal pardons

- **Federal offenses only**: "The President's authority to grant clemency is limited to federal offenses and offenses prosecuted by the United States Attorney for the District of Columbia in the name of the United States in the D.C. Superior Court." "A person who wishes to seek a pardon or a commutation of sentence for a state offense should contact the authorities of the state in which the conviction occurred." Source: https://www.justice.gov/pardon/frequently-asked-questions
- **The state mirror**: "The Governor of California cannot grant a pardon for a conviction from another state or a federal proceeding." Source: https://www.gov.ca.gov/clemency/pardons/
- **Pardons are not expungement**: "Expungement is a judicial remedy that is rarely granted by the court and cannot be granted within the Department of Justice or by the President." "If you are seeking expungement of a federal offense, please contact the court of conviction." Source: https://www.justice.gov/pardon/frequently-asked-questions
- **Five-year waiting period (28 CFR 1.2)**: "No petition for pardon should be filed until the expiration of a waiting period of at least five years after the date of the release of the petitioner from confinement or, in case no prison sentence was imposed, until the expiration of a period of at least five years after the date of the conviction of the petitioner. Generally, no petition should be submitted by a person who is on probation, parole, or supervised release." Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/1.2
- **Justice Manual**: "The Department's regulations require a petitioner to wait a period of at least five years after conviction or release from confinement (whichever is later) before filing a pardon application." Source: https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-140000-pardon-attorney
- **How to apply**: file the clemency petition (forms on the OPA website) with the Office of the Pardon Attorney; questions to USPardon.Attorney@usdoj.gov. Reapply after a denial: commutation after 12 months; pardon after 24 months (per WebFetch summary). Source: https://www.justice.gov/pardon/frequently-asked-questions
- **No guarantee of a decision**: "there is no guarantee that an application submitted during an administration will be decided by that President." Source: https://www.justice.gov/pardon/frequently-asked-questions
- **Note**: the C.D. Cal. federal court FAQ mentions a seven-year wait for some serious offenses. **[UNCERTAIN/likely outdated: current 28 CFR 1.2 says five years with no 7-year tier]** https://www.cacp.uscourts.gov/faq/general/30

## 4. State pardons: who grants them

- **Texas (governor on board recommendation)**: "the Governor may grant clemency upon written recommendation of a majority of the Board"; "The Governor makes the final decision to grant clemency. The Board may only recommend or not recommend that the Governor grant clemency." Figures: in FY2023 the Board recommended 3 of 108 noncapital clemency requests (3%). In FY2025 it recommended "9 noncapital clemency requests out of 130 applications considered, or 7%." Sources: https://www.tdcj.texas.gov/bpp/publications/FY_2023_Annual_Statistical_Report.pdf (p. 21-22); https://www.tdcj.texas.gov/bpp/publications/FY_2025_Annual_Statistical_Report.pdf (p. 21-22)
- **Georgia (an independent board grants pardons, not the governor)**: in FY2025, "874 pardon and restoration of rights applications were received and 436 were granted (all types)". Breakdown: 169 pardons without firearms restored, 164 pardons with firearms restored, 80 restorations of civil and political rights, 23 restorations with firearms; 151 cases denied. Applicants must be crime-free for 5 years after completing all sentences (10 years for sex-registry offenses). Source: https://pap.georgia.gov/document/document/pardons-paroles-ar-2025-d12-1pdf/download (p. 28)
- **Pennsylvania (Board of Pardons recommends, Governor decides)**: "If a majority of the Board votes in favor of the application, the Board will recommend that clemency be granted by the Governor." "For applicants with life sentences or death sentences, the Board must vote unanimously." "The Governor may approve or disapprove any favorable recommendation submitted by the Board." Source: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/bop/process
- **California (Governor, with Board of Parole Hearings investigation)**: the Board of Parole Hearings "investigates pardon applications." The Governor "cannot grant pardons to applicants with more than one felony conviction without the approval of the California Supreme Court." Source: https://www.gov.ca.gov/clemency/pardons/

## 5. Federal records: no general expungement statute

- **Federal court FAQ (C.D. Cal.)**: "Federal convictions cannot be expunged. However, you may apply for a Presidential Pardon." Source: https://www.cacp.uscourts.gov/faq/general/30
- **CRS Legal Sidebar LSB10413 (Peter G. Berris, Feb. 27, 2020)**: "few such laws exist at the federal level." "Federal law permits expungement in three primary areas": (1) inaccurate records (Privacy Act); (2) records from an invalid, unlawful or unconstitutional process; (3) specific statutes. "Federal appellate courts disagree on whether they have authority to consider equitable expungement claims." The 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th, 8th, 9th and 10th Circuits say no jurisdiction; the 4th, 5th and D.C. Circuits allow it in exceptional circumstances (per WebFetch summary). Source: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB10413.html (mirror of CRS; official copy: https://crsreports.congress.gov/product/pdf/LSB/LSB10413)
- **9th Cir., United States v. Crowell, 374 F.3d 790 (2004)**: a search snippet says Congress "has not expressly granted to the federal courts a general power to expunge criminal records." **[UNCERTAIN: page blocked by bot check; quote not read directly]** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/374/790/505889/
- **Narrow exception, 18 U.S.C. 3607(c)**: this applies to simple possession under 21 U.S.C. 844. The person must have had no prior drug conviction and no earlier 3607 disposition, and must complete pre-judgment probation of up to one year. If the person "was less than twenty-one years old at the time of the offense, the court shall enter an expungement order upon the application of such person." The person is then not guilty of perjury for failing "to recite or acknowledge such arrests." Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/3607
- **Other narrow statutes**: for example, 10 U.S.C. 1565(e) (DNA records after an overturned military conviction), as cited in a 6th Cir. opinion and CRS. **[Not read directly]** https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/14a0136p-06.pdf
- **State relief does not bind federal agencies**: "state orders do not apply to federal databases." (Illinois Legal Aid) Source: https://www.illinoislegalaid.org/legal-information/after-your-expungement-or-sealing-case-decided-common-questions
