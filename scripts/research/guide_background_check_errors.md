# Expunged or wrong record showing on a background check — fact sheet

Research date: November 20, 2026. Every bullet is taken from the source linked after it, opened on that date. This is general information, not legal advice.

In this sheet "background check company" means a consumer reporting agency under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), and "report" means a consumer report.

## 1. What the FCRA requires of background check companies

### Accuracy (15 U.S.C. 1681e(b))

- "Whenever a consumer reporting agency prepares a consumer report it shall follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of the information concerning the individual about whom the report relates." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681e]
- The FTC tells tenant screening companies that signs their procedures may not be reasonable include reports with "criminal records that have been expunged or otherwise sealed", records belonging to someone with a different middle name or date of birth, several entries for the same offence, and court cases listed without their outcome. [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/what-tenant-background-screening-companies-need-know-about-fair-credit-reporting-act]

### Public records used for employment (15 U.S.C. 1681k)

- A company that reports public record information "likely to have an adverse effect upon a consumer's ability to obtain employment" must do one of two things: (1) tell the person, at the time it reports the information, that it is being reported and to whom; or (2) "maintain strict procedures designed to insure" that the information "is complete and up to date". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681k]
- Records of arrests, indictments, convictions, suits, tax liens and outstanding judgments count as up to date "if the current public record status of the item at the time of the report is reported". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681k]

### Time limits on old information (15 U.S.C. 1681c)

- Records of arrest, civil suits and civil judgments cannot be reported once they are more than seven years old (or until the statute of limitations runs out, if longer). Other adverse items older than seven years cannot be reported either, "other than records of convictions of crimes". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c]
- These limits do not apply to reports for a job with an annual salary of $75,000 or more, credit of $150,000 or more, or life insurance of $150,000 or more. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c]
- The CFPB tells consumers there is no federal time limit on reporting convictions, though some states have their own rules. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]

### Disputes and reinvestigation (15 U.S.C. 1681i)

- If a person disputes the completeness or accuracy of an item directly with the company, the company "shall, free of charge, conduct a reasonable reinvestigation" and either record the current status of the item or delete it "before the end of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the agency receives the notice of the dispute". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- The 30 days can be extended by up to 15 more days if the person sends relevant information during the 30-day period. The extension does not apply if, during the 30 days, the item is found inaccurate or incomplete or cannot be verified. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- Within 5 business days of receiving the dispute, the company must notify whoever supplied the disputed information and pass on all relevant information from the person. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- The company "shall review and consider all relevant information submitted by the consumer". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- If an item is "found to be inaccurate or incomplete or cannot be verified", the company must promptly delete or modify it and notify the supplier of the information. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- The company must give written notice of the results within 5 business days after finishing, including a copy of the report as revised and notice of the right to add a statement of dispute. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- A deleted item may not be put back unless the supplier certifies it is complete and accurate, and the company must tell the person in writing within 5 business days of putting it back. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- The company may end a reinvestigation if it reasonably decides the dispute is "frivolous or irrelevant", including where the person has not given enough information to investigate. It must tell the person within 5 business days, with reasons and what information is needed. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- If the reinvestigation does not resolve the dispute, the person may file a brief statement setting out the dispute; the company may limit it to 100 words if it helps the person write a clear summary. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- After a deletion or a dispute notation, the person can ask the company to notify anyone they name who received the report in the past two years for employment purposes, or in the past six months for any other purpose. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]

## 2. What employers must do

### Before getting a report (15 U.S.C. 1681b(b)(2))

- An employer may not obtain a report for employment purposes unless it has made "a clear and conspicuous disclosure ... in writing ... in a document that consists solely of the disclosure" and the person "has authorized in writing" the report. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681b]

### Before rejecting someone because of a report (15 U.S.C. 1681b(b)(3))

- "before taking any adverse action based in whole or in part on the report", the employer must give the person "(i) a copy of the report; and (ii) a description in writing of the rights of the consumer". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681b]
- The FTC tells employers this means a notice that includes a copy of the report relied on and a copy of "A Summary of Your Rights Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act", and that advance notice lets the person review the report and point out errors. [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-employers-need-know]
- A different rule applies only to certain transportation jobs regulated by the Secretary of Transportation or a state transportation agency where the person applied only by mail, phone, computer or similar means: the employer must give notice within 3 business days of taking the action. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681b]

### After rejecting someone (15 U.S.C. 1681m)

- Anyone who takes adverse action based wholly or partly on a report must: give notice of the action (orally, in writing or electronically); give the name, address and phone number of the company that supplied the report; state that the company did not make the decision and cannot give the specific reasons; and give notice of the right to a free copy of the report within 60 days and the right to dispute its accuracy or completeness with the company. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681m]
- The company must provide that copy free of charge if the person asks no later than 60 days after receiving the adverse action notice. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681j]
- The same after-the-fact notice applies to landlords: turning someone down, charging more rent, requiring a co-signer or a bigger deposit because of a report all require an adverse action notice. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]

## 3. How to dispute an error: steps, what to send, timelines

- **Step 1 — Get the report.** The employer must hand over a copy before acting on it. You can also ask the background check company for a free copy within 60 days of the employer's decision. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/employer-background-checks-your-rights]
- If possible, ask for the name of the background check company when you give consent, so you can request a copy of that report. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/documents/10037/cfpb_background-screening-report_handout_2021-08.pdf]
- **Step 2 — Review it.** For criminal records, the CFPB says to check that misdemeanors and felonies are listed accurately, that one charge does not appear several times, and that expunged or removed records do not appear. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]
- **Step 3 — Dispute in writing with the background check company.** "First, inform the background screening company in writing of the error or information you are disputing." You can tell the employer about the mistake as well. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/documents/10037/cfpb_background-screening-report_handout_2021-08.pdf]
- **What to send.** Include documents that show the accurate or complete information. Send copies, not originals. The CFPB suggests certified mail with return receipt so you have a record. You may need to get the underlying criminal history record from the courthouse or state repository. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/documents/10037/cfpb_background-screening-report_handout_2021-08.pdf]
- The FTC likewise says to explain the mistakes, ask for them to be fixed, and include any supporting documents. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/employer-background-checks-your-rights]
- For a tenant report, the FTC gives the example of "paperwork showing how a criminal case or eviction was resolved". [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]
- **Also contact the source.** The CFPB says to contact both the background check company and whoever reported the incorrect information; for criminal or eviction record errors you can also contact the court. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]
- **Timeline.** The company "generally has 30 days (an additional 15 days in some circumstances) from the time it receives your dispute to investigate and respond". Once it finishes, it has 5 business days to notify you. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/documents/10037/cfpb_background-screening-report_handout_2021-08.pdf]
- The CFPB notes some states set shorter deadlines. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-my-rental-application-is-denied-because-of-a-tenant-screening-report-en-2105/]
- **Step 4 — Check the result and get it to the employer.** After the report is revised, check that the errors are gone, and ask the company to send the corrected report to the employer and tell the employer about the mistake. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/employer-background-checks-your-rights]
- **Step 5 — If it is not fixed.** You can submit a complaint to the CFPB (consumerfinance.gov/complaint) or the FTC. The CFPB handout adds: "You can also file a lawsuit." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/documents/10037/cfpb_background-screening-report_handout_2021-08.pdf]
- If an employer got your report without permission or rejected you without the required notices, the FTC asks you to report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/employer-background-checks-your-rights]
- A 2022 CFPB review of complaints about tenant screening found that people who tried to correct errors sometimes saw the same wrong information on later reports. (The CFPB page is now marked as archived content.) [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-reports-highlight-problems-with-tenant-background-checks/]

## 4. Remedies and the time limit to sue

What the statute says, without any prediction about a particular case:

- **Willful violations (15 U.S.C. 1681n).** A person who "willfully fails to comply" is liable for: "any actual damages sustained by the consumer as a result of the failure or damages of not less than $100 and not more than $1,000"; "such amount of punitive damages as the court may allow"; and "in the case of any successful action to enforce any liability under this section, the costs of the action together with reasonable attorney's fees as determined by the court". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681n]
- **Negligent violations (15 U.S.C. 1681o).** A person who "is negligent in failing to comply" is liable for "any actual damages sustained by the consumer as a result of the failure" and, "in the case of any successful action", the costs of the action together with reasonable attorney's fees as determined by the court. There is no fixed minimum amount and no punitive damages in this section. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681o]
- **What the fee wording means.** In both sections, costs and attorney's fees are awarded by the court only "in the case of any successful action", and the amount is "as determined by the court". The statute does not say a case costs nothing to bring and does not promise any result. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681n] [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681o]
- **Fees can also go the other way.** If the court finds that an unsuccessful pleading, motion or other paper "was filed in bad faith or for purposes of harassment", it "shall award to the prevailing party attorney's fees reasonable in relation to the work expended in responding". This appears in both sections. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681n] [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681o]
- **Time limit (15 U.S.C. 1681p).** An action must be brought "not later than the earlier of — (1) 2 years after the date of discovery by the plaintiff of the violation that is the basis for such liability; or (2) 5 years after the date on which the violation that is the basis for such liability occurs." It may be brought in a US district court "without regard to the amount in controversy, or in any other court of competent jurisdiction". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681p]
- The CFPB summarises: a person "may be able to sue" under the FCRA and state law; if they win a federal FCRA suit they "may be able to recover damages and attorney fees"; statutes of limitation apply. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-my-rental-application-is-denied-because-of-a-tenant-screening-report-en-2105/]

## 5. What the CFPB and FTC have said about sealed or expunged records

- **FTC business guidance (current page).** Reports that include "criminal records that have been expunged or otherwise sealed" are listed as a sign that a screening company may not be following reasonable procedures. [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/what-tenant-background-screening-companies-need-know-about-fair-credit-reporting-act]
- **FTC consumer advice (page dated August 2025).** Criminal or eviction records that were sealed or expunged are listed as a common type of error on tenant background checks. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]
- **CFPB consumer page (last modified January 30, 2024).** Tells renters that sealed or expunged evictions should not appear, and expunged or removed criminal records should not appear. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]
- **FTC and CFPB enforcement (October 2023).** The agencies alleged a tenant screening company "failed to put in place reasonable procedures to prevent eviction records that had been sealed" from appearing in its reports; the settlement was $15 million ($11 million for consumers, $4 million penalty). [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/10/ftc-cfpb-settlement-require-trans-union-pay-15-million-over-charges-it-failed-ensure-accuracy-tenant]
- **CFPB advisory opinion (January 23, 2024) — since withdrawn.** The opinion said a company is not using reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy if it lacks procedures to ensure it "does not report information that is duplicative or that has been expunged, sealed, or otherwise legally restricted from public access", and to include existing disposition information when reporting arrests, charges, eviction proceedings or other court filings. [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-01-23/html/2024-00788.htm]
- **Withdrawal (May 12, 2025).** The CFPB withdrew that advisory opinion ("Fair Credit Reporting; Background Screening, 89 FR 4171") along with many other guidance documents, including its 2021 opinion on name-only matching. The notice says the withdrawn guidance "should not be enforced or otherwise relied upon by the Bureau" while under review, and that "Such withdrawal is not necessarily final." [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-05-12/html/2025-08286.htm]
- What this means for the guide: the statute (sections 1681e(b), 1681k and 1681i) is unchanged, and the FTC and CFPB consumer pages above still say sealed or expunged records should not appear. The CFPB's more detailed 2024 interpretation is no longer in effect as Bureau guidance. [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-05-12/html/2025-08286.htm]
