## 1. Texas: what deferred adjudication is (Code of Criminal Procedure ch. 42A, subch. C)

Source for this section: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CR/htm/CR.42A.htm (read via https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CR/htm/CR.42A.htm)

- Art. 42A.101(a): "if in the judge's opinion the best interest of society and the defendant will be served, the judge may, after receiving a plea of guilty or nolo contendere, hearing the evidence, and finding that it substantiates the defendant's guilt, defer further proceedings without entering an adjudication of guilt and place the defendant on deferred adjudication community supervision."
- Art. 42A.103: felony deferred adjudication "may not exceed 10 years" (for certain sex offenses, not less than five years); misdemeanor "may not exceed two years." The judge may extend under 42A.753 or 42A.757.
- Art. 42A.104(a): the judge may impose a fine and "any reasonable condition" that could be imposed on regular probation, "including: (1) confinement; and (2) mental health treatment."
- Art. 42A.102(b): deferred adjudication is not available for certain offenses, including: intoxication offenses under Penal Code 49.045, 49.05, 49.061, 49.065, 49.07 (intoxication assault), 49.08 (intoxication manslaughter); trafficking (20A.02, 20A.03); DWI/BWI (49.04/49.06) where the person held a commercial license or permit or had alcohol concentration of 0.15 or more; offenses enhanced under 49.09; certain repeat drug-free-zone offenses; 481.1123(d)-(f); 21.02, 21.03, certain 22.021; murder (19.02) unless the judge finds the defendant did not cause the death, did not intend to kill and did not anticipate a life would be taken; and repeat sex offenses listed in (2).
- Art. 42A.108(a)-(b): on violation the defendant may be arrested; the defendant "is entitled to a hearing limited to a determination by the court of whether the court will proceed with an adjudication of guilt on the original charge."
- Art. 42A.110(a): "After an adjudication of guilt, all proceedings, including assessment of punishment, pronouncement of sentence, granting of community supervision, and defendant's appeal, continue as if the adjudication of guilt had not been deferred." (i.e., if adjudicated, the full punishment range applies.)
- Art. 42A.106(a): "Except as provided by Section 552.142, Government Code, a record in the custody of the court clerk regarding a case in which a defendant is granted deferred adjudication community supervision is not confidential."
- Art. 42A.106(b): before placing a defendant on deferred adjudication, "the court shall inform the defendant of the defendant's right to receive or petition the court for an order of nondisclosure of criminal history record information under Subchapter E-1, Chapter 411, Government Code," unless ineligible because of the offense or criminal history.
- Art. 42A.111(a): "On expiration of a period of deferred adjudication community supervision imposed under this subchapter, if the judge has not proceeded to an adjudication of guilt, the judge shall dismiss the proceedings against the defendant and discharge the defendant."
- Art. 42A.111(b): the judge may dismiss and discharge early "if, in the judge's opinion, the best interest of society and the defendant will be served," but not for an offense requiring sex offender registration.
- Art. 42A.111(c): "Except as provided by Section 12.42(g), Penal Code, a dismissal and discharge under this article may not be considered a conviction for the purposes of disqualifications or disabilities imposed by law for conviction of an offense."
- Art. 42A.111(c-1): subject to (d), the offense "may not be used as grounds for denying issuance of a professional or occupational license or certificate" to someone otherwise qualified.
- Art. 42A.111(d)(1): "on conviction of a subsequent offense, the fact that the defendant previously has received deferred adjudication community supervision is admissible before the court or jury for consideration on the issue of penalty." (d)(2)-(4): certain licensing agencies may still consider it (child-care licensing under Human Resources Code ch. 42; sex-offender-treatment licensing; and licensing agencies where the offense is listed in 42A.054(a), is a Chapter 21 or 43 offense, relates to the licensed activity, the job involves direct contact with children, or the license is under Occupations Code ch. 1701 [peace officers]).
- Art. 42A.111(e): the judge who dismisses must give the defendant a copy of the dismissal and discharge order and, if the defendant is or may become eligible for nondisclosure, shall (as applicable) grant the order (text read was cut off after "(A) grant an order of nondisclosure of criminal history record information").

## 2. Texas: orders of nondisclosure (Government Code ch. 411, subch. E-1)

Statute source: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm (read via https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/GV/htm/GV.411.htm)
OCA overview: "An Overview of Orders of Nondisclosure," Texas Office of Court Administration, header "REVISED AUGUST 2026": https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf

### What it is
- OCA: "An order of nondisclosure is a court order prohibiting public entities, including courts, clerks of the court, law enforcement agencies, and prosecutorial offices, from disclosing certain criminal records." https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf
- OCA: "An order of nondisclosure legally frees you from having to disclose certain information about your criminal history in response to questions on job applications." The order "applies to a particular criminal offense" but "you may obtain multiple orders for multiple offenses." https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf
- Gov't Code 411.0755: a person with a nondisclosure order "is not required in any application for employment, information, or licensing to state that the person has been the subject of any criminal proceeding related to the information that is the subject of the order." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Gov't Code 411.0715: for nondisclosure purposes, a person is considered placed on deferred adjudication if (1) they pleaded guilty or nolo contendere; (2) the judge deferred proceedings without adjudicating guilt and placed them under supervision; and (3) "at the end of the period of supervision the judge dismissed the proceedings and discharged the person." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm

### Basic bars for everyone (411.074)
- 411.074(a): only if, from sentencing/placement through any waiting period, "the person is not convicted of or placed on deferred adjudication community supervision for any offense other than a traffic offense that is punishable by fine only." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.074(b): no order if the person requests it for, or was ever previously convicted of or placed on deferred adjudication for: (A) an offense requiring sex offender registration; (B) aggravated kidnapping (20.04); (C) Penal Code 19.02 (murder), 19.03 (capital murder), 20A.02, 20A.03 (trafficking), 22.04 (injury to a child, elderly or disabled person), 22.041 (abandoning or endangering a child), 25.07, 25.072 (violating protective orders/bond conditions), or 42.072 (stalking); or (D) any other offense involving family violence (Family Code 71.004); or (2) the court made an affirmative finding the offense involved family violence. https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm ; same list in OCA overview pp. 3-4.

### Automatic nondisclosure for certain first-time nonviolent misdemeanors (411.072)
- Applies only to someone placed on deferred adjudication for a misdemeanor other than DWI/BWI (49.04/49.06) or a misdemeanor under Penal Code chapters 20, 21, 22, 25, 42, 43, 46 or 71, and where no 42A.105(f) "not in the best interest of justice" finding was filed, and who "has never been previously convicted of or placed on deferred adjudication community supervision for another offense other than a traffic offense that is punishable by fine only." 411.072(a). https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.072(b): if such a person receives a discharge and dismissal and meets 411.074, the court "shall issue an order of nondisclosure" (1) "at the time the court discharges and dismisses the proceedings against the person, if the discharge and dismissal occurs on or after the 180th day after the date the court placed the person on deferred adjudication community supervision; or (2) as soon as practicable on or after the 180th day ... if the discharge and dismissal occurred before that date." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.072(c): "The person shall present to the court any evidence necessary to establish that the person is eligible ... The person must pay a $28 fee to the clerk of the court before the court issues the order." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.072(d): someone shut out only because of a 42A.105(f) finding may petition under 411.0725. https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- CCP 42A.105(f): for misdemeanors outside those chapters, the judge "shall make an affirmative finding" if the judge "determines that it is not in the best interest of justice that the defendant receive an automatic order of nondisclosure under Section 411.072." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CR/htm/CR.42A.htm
- OCA: ineligible under 411.072 "if you received a discharge and dismissal prior to September 1, 2017." No petition is needed; if eligible and the court failed to consider you, "you may present evidence ... by completing and submitting this letter to the court and paying a $28 fee." Fee waiver: Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs (Tex. R. Civ. P. 145). https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf (pp. 10-11)
- OCA 2025 District Court Civil Filing Fees schedule (revised 11/03/2025): "Fee for Orders of Nondisclosure for Certain Nonviolent Misdemeanors (pursuant to Gov't Code § 411.072)" Amount: $28.00. Note: "Although SB 41 (87R) amended § 411.0745(b), Tex. Gov't Code, to repeal the $28 fee that must accompany a petition for an order of nondisclosure, it did not repeal the $28 fee that a person must pay before an automatic order of nondisclosure issues under § 411.072." The local and state consolidated civil fees "do not apply to automatic orders of nondisclosure issued under § 411.072." https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461496/dc-civ-fil-fees-2025.pdf
- TexasLawHelp (Texas Legal Services Center; last legal review Sept 3, 2025, last edited Feb 12, 2026), 411.072 steps: "There is a $28.00 fee. Ask the clerk if you need to set a hearing. Not all judges require one." "you can file the letter with the court clerk at the time of dismissal or 180 days after you started deferred adjudication -- whichever is later. The dismissal date is usually later." "(That is 180 days after you started deferred adjudication, not when you completed it.)" "not all courts are set up to automatically give disclosure orders even if there is a dismissal hearing. You may need to return to court to get your order if you do not get it at the time of dismissal." https://texaslawhelp.org/guide/how-to-ask-for-a-nondisclosure-order-seal-your-criminal-record

### Petition-based nondisclosure after deferred adjudication: felonies and other misdemeanors (411.0725)
- Applies to people placed on deferred adjudication who are not eligible under 411.072 and whose offense was not DWI/BWI. 411.0725(a). https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- After discharge and dismissal, and meeting 411.074, "the person may petition the court that placed the person on deferred adjudication community supervision." 411.0725(b). Prior record does not bar it except as provided in 411.074 (411.0725(c)). https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.0725(d): "After notice to the state, an opportunity for a hearing, and a determination that the person is entitled to file the petition and issuance of the order is in the best interest of justice, the court shall issue an order..." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Waiting periods, 411.0725(e): (1) immediately on discharge and dismissal for a misdemeanor not in (2); (2) "the second anniversary of the discharge and dismissal" for a misdemeanor under Penal Code ch. 20, 21, 22, 25, 42, 43 or 46; (3) "the fifth anniversary of the discharge and dismissal, if the offense ... was a felony." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.0745(a)-(b): petition may be filed "in person, electronically, or by mail"; "The petition must be accompanied by payment of a fee that generally applies to the filing of a civil case." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.0745(e): the court gives the state notice and an opportunity for a hearing; the court "shall hold a hearing" unless the state does not request one "before the 45th day after the date on which the state receives notice" and the court determines the person is entitled and the order is in the best interest of justice. https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Civil filing fee amount: OCA's 2025 County Court Civil Filing Fees schedule (revised 10/27/2025) describes the fee for an original civil case as "(Currently, $350.00)". https://txcourts.gov/media/1461494/cc-civ-fil-fees-2025.pdf . OCA's district court schedule lists the two statewide required fees for a new civil case: local consolidated civil fee $213.00 and state consolidated civil fee $137.00 (= $350). Both schedules also list "Statewide Optional Filing Fees" that counties may add. https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461496/dc-civ-fil-fees-2025.pdf
  - NOT VERIFIED: a current county-specific total for a 411.0725 petition. Amount in practice can be higher than $350 due to optional local fees.
- TexasLawHelp 411.0725 steps: "File the petition and unsigned order with the clerk of the court that placed you on deferred adjudication. Ask the clerk if you need to set a hearing. If you are low income, you can file a Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs (fee waiver)." "You must give the prosecutor a copy of your filed petition." "If the prosecutor challenges, talk to an attorney. If there is a hearing, you must attend." "Be prepared to explain why sealing your record is in the best interests of justice." Step 6: "You may want to tell private background-check companies that have your information to remove your record from their database." https://texaslawhelp.org/guide/how-to-ask-for-a-nondisclosure-order-seal-your-criminal-record

### DWI deferred adjudication (411.0726) (brief)
- Deferred adjudication for DWI/BWI became possible under 42A.102 except CDL holders / 0.15+ BAC / enhanced. For nondisclosure under 411.0726: first-time offender, no accident involving another person, no CDL, BAC under 0.15, not enhanced; waiting period "two years following your discharge and dismissal." OCA overview pp. 12-14. https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf

### After the order (411.075, 411.0765, 411.0851)
- 411.075(a): the clerk sends the order to DPS Crime Records Service "Not later than the 15th business day after the date an order ... is issued." (b): DPS "Not later than 10 business days after receipt ... shall seal" its records and send the order to law enforcement agencies, courts, prosecutors etc., central federal depositories, and "private entities that purchase criminal history record information from the department." (d): agencies in (b)(1) must seal within 30 business days of receiving it. https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- OCA: DPS seals, "not delete," and "some agencies, including law enforcement agencies, will still have access to the information, although the information is sealed." https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1438062/overview-of-orders-of-nondisclosure.pdf (pp. 23-24)
- 411.0765(a): criminal justice agencies may disclose sealed information only to other criminal justice agencies, "for criminal justice or regulatory licensing purposes," to listed entities, to the person, or to comply with federal law. (b) lists entities including: State Board for Educator Certification; school districts, charter and private schools; Texas Medical Board; Board of Law Examiners; State Bar; Department of Family and Protective Services; Texas Board of Nursing; hospitals; Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation; Texas Department of Insurance; State Board of Pharmacy; banks, credit unions and mortgage bankers (for employees/applicants); and certain hazardous-materials critical-infrastructure employers. https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- 411.0851(a): a private entity that "compiles and disseminates for compensation criminal history record information shall destroy and may not disseminate any information ... with respect to which the entity has received notice that" an expunction or nondisclosure order has been issued. (c): an entity that violates this "is liable for any damages," plus court costs and reasonable attorney's fees for a prevailing plaintiff. https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- TexasLawHelp article (composed by Texas OCA; last legal review Sept 3, 2025): "You can also contact the Foundation for Continuing Justice and ask them to send copies of the nondisclosure order to private companies that might have your record on file. This is a free service." https://texaslawhelp.org/article/nondisclosure-orders-and-sealing-your-criminal-record-in-texas
- TexasLawHelp guide FAQ: nondisclosure orders "keep your sealed information private from landlords, lenders, school admissions, and most employers"; "law enforcement, the federal government, and most professional licensing entities can still view your sealed record. Certain private employers, such as banks and companies involved in critical infrastructure, may also view..." "You can deny the offense except in future criminal proceedings." https://texaslawhelp.org/guide/how-to-ask-for-a-nondisclosure-order-seal-your-criminal-record
- TexasLawHelp article: "Do not get overwhelmed by how many options there are. The Fresh Start app or the Nondisclosure Order Prep Guide can help you figure it out." Forms are downloadable from its "Nondisclosure Orders - Seal Your Record" page, including the fee waiver "Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs or an Appeal Bond." https://texaslawhelp.org/article/nondisclosure-orders-and-sealing-your-criminal-record-in-texas ; https://texaslawhelp.org/guide/how-to-ask-for-a-nondisclosure-order-seal-your-criminal-record
- TexasLawHelp lists free help options, including the Texas Fair Defense Project (512-637-5220), The Beacon, UT Texas Law Expunction Project and others, plus TexasLawHelp's online chat and Legal Help Directory. https://texaslawhelp.org/article/nondisclosure-orders-and-sealing-your-criminal-record-in-texas

## 3. Texas: expunction vs. nondisclosure (CCP ch. 55A, effective Jan. 1, 2025)

Source: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CR/htm/CR.55A.htm (read via https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CR/htm/CR.55A.htm)

- Chapter 55A ("Expunction of Criminal Records") was added by Acts 2023, H.B. 4504, eff. January 1, 2025 (replacing former ch. 55).
- Art. 55A.051 (Subchapter B, "Special Circumstances Requiring Mandatory Expunction") applies to an arrested person if "(1) the person has been released; (2) the charge, if any, has not resulted in a final conviction and is no longer pending; and (3) there was no court-ordered community supervision under Chapter 42A for the offense, other than for a Class C misdemeanor."
  - Meaning: deferred adjudication is court-ordered community supervision under ch. 42A, so a deferred adjudication case (other than a Class C misdemeanor) generally does not qualify for expunction under these provisions. (Our reading of the text; no official page read that states it in these words. TexasLawHelp: "Expunction options are more limited.")
- Art. 55A.053: expunction where an indictment/information was dismissed because the person completed a veterans treatment court, mental health court, or pretrial intervention program, or because of mistake/absence of probable cause, or the charge was void. (Pretrial diversion is different from deferred adjudication.)
- Art. 55A.401: when an expunction order is final, "the release, maintenance, dissemination, or use of the expunged records and files for any purpose is prohibited" and "the person arrested may deny the occurrence of the arrest and the existence of the expunction order," except under oath in a criminal proceeding, where the person "may state only that the matter in question has been expunged."
- Art. 55A.153: records of an arrest made on a warrant for violating community supervision (42A.751(b)) may not be expunged.
- TexasLawHelp: "Expunction completely removes your offense from your record. That makes it unviewable in almost all cases. ... A nondisclosure order only seals an offense on your record from unauthorized viewers. Expunction options are more limited." https://texaslawhelp.org/guide/how-to-ask-for-a-nondisclosure-order-seal-your-criminal-record

## 4. Other states

### Oklahoma: deferred sentence / deferred judgment (22 O.S. 991c) — amended eff. Nov 1, 2025
Source: https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=439118
- (A): "Upon a verdict or plea of guilty or upon a plea of nolo contendere, but before a judgment of guilt, the court may, without entering a judgment of guilt and with the consent of the defendant, defer further proceedings upon the specific conditions prescribed by the court not to exceed a seven-year period". Conditions can include restitution, costs, community service, up to 90 days county jail, supervision up to 18 months with a $40/month supervision fee (waivable for indigence).
- (D): on completion and payment, "the defendant shall be discharged without a court judgment of guilt, and the court shall order the verdict or plea of guilty or plea of nolo contendere to be expunged from the record and the charge shall be dismissed with prejudice to any further action." The name is deleted from the docket and the public index expunged; "Records expunged pursuant to this subsection shall be sealed to the public but not to law enforcement agencies for law enforcement purposes" and are admissible in a later prosecution to prove a prior deferred judgment. Persons qualifying under 22 O.S. 18 may also petition to have the filing and dismissal expunged.
- (H): only for defendants not previously convicted of a felony and who have not received more than one felony deferred judgment in the previous ten years (DA may request waiver). (I): not for sex offenses requiring registration.
Source: 22 O.S. 18 (amended eff. July 1, 2026): https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=440214
- (A)(8): misdemeanor dismissed after successful deferred judgment, never convicted of a felony, nothing pending, "at least one (1) year has passed since the charge was dismissed."
- (A)(9): nonviolent felony (not listed in 57 O.S. 571) dismissed after deferred judgment, no felony conviction, nothing pending, "at least five (5) years have passed since the charge was dismissed."
- (A)(14): up to two felonies dismissed after deferred judgment (not 21 O.S. 13.1 offenses or registrable sex offenses), "at least ten (10) years have passed."
- (D): records expunged under (8), (9), (14) are partially sealed: "not available to the public but remain available to law enforcement agencies for law enforcement purposes."

### Florida: withhold of adjudication (Fla. Stat. 948.01) and sealing (943.059) — 2025 statutes
- 948.01(2): the court "may either adjudge the defendant to be guilty or stay and withhold the adjudication of guilt. In either case, the court shall stay and withhold the imposition of sentence upon the defendant and shall place a felony defendant upon probation." https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/948.01
- 943.059(1): a person may petition to seal if, among other things, they have "never, before the date the application for a certificate of eligibility is filed, been adjudicated guilty in this state of a criminal offense" (and certain juvenile adjudications), have "not been adjudicated guilty of ... any of the acts stemming from the arrest" to be sealed, are no longer under court supervision for it, and have never had a prior sealing or expunction; and the record is not ineligible under 943.0584. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/943.059
- 943.059(2): must first get a certificate of eligibility from FDLE; "$75 processing fee," unless waived; certificate valid for 12 months. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/943.059
- NOT READ: 943.0584 (list of ineligible offenses) and 943.0585 (expunction).

### North Carolina: conditional discharge and deferred prosecution (G.S. 15A-1341)
Source: https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_15A/GS_15A-1341.html
- (a4) Conditional discharge: "Whenever a person pleads guilty to or is found guilty of a Class H or I felony or a misdemeanor, the court may, on joint motion of the defendant and the prosecutor, and without entering a judgment of guilt and with the consent of the person, defer further proceedings and place the person on probation". Requires: victims notified; no prior felony or misdemeanor involving moral turpitude; not previously on probation; unlikely to reoffend.
- (a1) Deferred prosecution for Class H or I felony or misdemeanor by written agreement with the prosecutor, approved by the court.
- (a6): "Upon fulfillment of the terms and conditions of a conditional discharge ... any plea or finding of guilty previously entered shall be withdrawn and the court shall discharge the person and dismiss the proceedings against the person."
- Not available for impaired driving (G.S. 20-138.1) under (a).

### Louisiana: Code of Criminal Procedure art. 893 (felonies)
Source: https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=112882 (amended through Acts 2026, No. 520, eff. May 29, 2026)
- 893(E)(1)(a): the court "may defer, in whole or in part, the imposition of a sentence after conviction of a first offense noncapital felony" and place the defendant on probation. Note: unlike Texas, a conviction is entered first.
- 893(E)(1)(b): not for crimes of violence designated under art. 890.3, sex offenses involving a child under 17, certain drug offenses punishable by more than ten years, or violations of R.S. 40:966(A)-970(A).
- 893(E)(2): if probation is satisfactory, "the court may set the conviction aside and dismiss the prosecution. The dismissal of the prosecution shall have the same effect as acquittal, except that the conviction may be considered as a first offense and provide the basis for subsequent prosecution of the party as a habitual offender". "Dismissal under this Paragraph shall occur only twice with respect to any person."
- 893(G): "Nothing contained in this Section shall be construed as being a basis for destruction of records of the arrest and prosecution of any person convicted of a felony."
- NOT READ: art. 894 (misdemeanor counterpart).
Source: art. 978: https://legis.la.gov/legis/law.aspx?d=919670
- 978(A)(1): a person may move to expunge a felony arrest and conviction if "The conviction was set aside and the prosecution was dismissed pursuant to Article 893(E)." (B) bars expungement for crimes of violence (with exceptions), sex offenses/offenses against minors, certain drug offenses (but 893(E) drug dismissals may be expunged per (B)(3)(d)), and domestic abuse battery.

## 5. Federal law and background checks

- Immigration: 8 U.S.C. 1101(a)(48)(A): "The term 'conviction' means, with respect to an alien, a formal judgment of guilt of the alien entered by a court or, if adjudication of guilt has been withheld, where— (i) a judge or jury has found the alien guilty or the alien has entered a plea of guilty or nolo contendere or has admitted sufficient facts to warrant a finding of guilt, and (ii) the judge has ordered some form of punishment, penalty, or restraint on the alien's liberty to be imposed." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1101
- FCRA 15 U.S.C. 1681c(a)(2): consumer reports may not contain "records of arrest that, from date of entry, antedate the report by more than seven years or until the governing statute of limitations has expired, whichever is the longer period." (a)(5): "Any other adverse item of information, other than records of convictions of crimes which antedates the report by more than seven years." (b): these limits do not apply to jobs paying $75,000 or more a year, credit of $150,000+, or life insurance of $150,000+. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c
  - NOT VERIFIED: how background check companies classify a dismissed deferred adjudication (as a conviction or a non-conviction) under the FCRA. No official source read on this point.
- FCRA 15 U.S.C. 1681e(b): reporting agencies "shall follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681e
- FCRA 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A): on a dispute, the agency "shall, free of charge, conduct a reasonable reinvestigation" and record the current status or delete the item "before the end of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the agency receives the notice of the dispute"; may be extended (subparagraph B). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i
- CFPB (Review your rental background check): "If any of your criminal records was expunged or removed from public records, they should not appear on your report." "Arrests can remain on your report for seven years from the date of entry, unless a longer statute of limitations applies. There is no federal time limit for how long criminal convictions can stay on your report, although some states may have different rules." Dispute steps: contact both the background check company and whoever reported the information; describe the error in writing; include copies of supporting documents. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/
