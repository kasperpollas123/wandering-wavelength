# Fact sheet: How far back does a background check go?

Researched 2026-11-20. Each fact comes from a page read on that date, with its URL after it. Quotes are verbatim; everything else is a close paraphrase. Where a page could not be read, it says so. This is general information, not legal advice.

"Background check company" here means a consumer reporting agency under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). Section numbers: FCRA section 605 = 15 U.S.C. 1681c.

---

## 1. The federal 7-year rule (15 U.S.C. 1681c)

### What cannot be reported (1681c(a))
- "Except as authorized under subsection (b), no consumer reporting agency may make any consumer report containing any of the following items of information:" https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c
- (a)(2): "Civil suits, civil judgments, and records of arrest that, from date of entry, antedate the report by more than seven years or until the governing statute of limitations has expired, whichever is the longer period." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c
- (a)(5): "Any other adverse item of information, other than records of convictions of crimes which antedates the report by more than seven years." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c
- (a)(1): bankruptcy cases more than 10 years old. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c
- So: arrests and other non-conviction items are limited to 7 years; convictions are carved out of the 7-year limit, so federal law sets no time limit on reporting them.

### History of the conviction carve-out
- In 1998 (Pub. L. 105-347) Congress inserted ", other than records of convictions of crimes" into (a)(5) and struck the former paragraph that read: "Records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of crime which, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven years." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c (Amendments notes)

### The exceptions (1681c(b))
- "The provisions of paragraphs (1) through (5) of subsection (a) are not applicable in the case of any consumer credit report to be used in connection with— (1) a credit transaction involving, or which may reasonably be expected to involve, a principal amount of $150,000 or more; (2) the underwriting of life insurance involving, or which may reasonably be expected to involve, a face amount of $150,000 or more; or (3) the employment of any individual at an annual salary which equals, or which may reasonably be expected to equal $75,000, or more." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c
- The $75,000 figure replaced $20,000 in 1996 (Pub. L. 104-208). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c (Amendments notes)

### Agency summaries
- CFPB: "Arrests can remain on your report for seven years from the date of entry, unless a longer statute of limitations applies. There is no federal time limit for how long criminal convictions can stay on your report, although some states may have different rules." https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/
- FTC: "Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, tenant background check companies generally cannot report negative information if it's older than seven years. ... arrest records can't be included in a report after seven years. ... However, there is no time limit for criminal convictions." https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights

### When the 7 years starts for dismissed charges (CFPB 2024 advisory opinion, since withdrawn)
- The CFPB said some companies reporting dismissed cases "have used the disposition date to start the seven-year reporting period for records of arrests and other non-conviction criminal record information, rather than the ``date of entry'' for records of arrest or the date of the criminal charge", and so reported information longer than section 605(a) permits. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-01-23/html/2024-00788.htm
- It said "a non-conviction disposition of a criminal charge cannot be reported beyond the seven-year period that begins to run at the time of the charge," and that the reporting period "is not restarted or reopened by the occurrence of subsequent events." https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-01-23/html/2024-00788.htm
- Status: withdrawn May 12, 2025 (see section 6). The statute's own words ("from date of entry" for arrests) are unchanged. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-05-12/html/2025-08286.htm

## 2. Federal preemption of state limits (15 U.S.C. 1681t)
- "No requirement or prohibition may be imposed under the laws of any State— (1) with respect to any subject matter regulated under— ... (E) section 1681c of this title, relating to information contained in consumer reports, except that this subparagraph shall not apply to any State law in effect on September 30, 1996". https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681t
- Not researched: how courts have applied this to each state law below, or to state amendments after 1996. The guide should say this is a legal question for a lawyer.

## 3. State laws with their own time limits (all read 2026-11-20)

### California
- Civ. Code 1786.18(a) (investigative consumer reporting agencies): may not report "(7) Records of arrest, indictment, information, misdemeanor complaint, or conviction of a crime that, from the date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven years. These items of information shall no longer be reported if at any time it is learned that, in the case of a conviction, a full pardon has been granted or, in the case of an arrest, indictment, information, or misdemeanor complaint, a conviction did not result; except that records of arrest, indictment, information, or misdemeanor complaints may be reported pending pronouncement of judgment on the particular subject matter of those records." https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1786-18/
- 1786.18(b) exceptions: life insurance of $250,000 or more; or "If the investigative consumer report is to be used by an employer who is explicitly required by a governmental regulatory agency to check for records that are prohibited by subdivision (a)". No salary exception. https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1786-18/
- 1786.18(c): public-record arrest/conviction information must be verified "during the 30-day period ending on the date on which the report is furnished." https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1786-18/
- 1786.2(b): "consumer" means a person who has applied "for employment purposes, for insurance ..., or the hiring of a dwelling unit"; (c) "investigative consumer report" means a report in which information on character, reputation, personal characteristics or mode of living "is obtained through any means." https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1786-2/
- Civ. Code 1785.13(a)(6) (consumer credit reporting agencies) has the same 7-year wording for arrests and convictions. https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1785-13/
- Official site (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1786.18): NOT READ (blocked by a bot check). Text read on FindLaw, "Current as of January 01, 2026".

### Colorado
- C.R.S. 5-18-109(1): may not report "(e) Records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of a crime that, from the date of disposition, release, or parole, predate the report by more than seven years;" and "(e.5) Sealed records, expunged records, and records that did not result in a conviction;" https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-5-consumer-credit-code/co-rev-st-sect-5-18-109/
- 5-18-109(2): only exception is a credit transaction above the national conforming loan limit. No employment salary exception. https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-5-consumer-credit-code/co-rev-st-sect-5-18-109/
- Read on FindLaw ("Current as of January 01, 2025"); official Colorado source NOT READ.

### Kansas
- K.S.A. 50-704(a)(5): "records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of crime which, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven (7) years". https://www.ksrevisor.org/statutes/chapters/ch50/050_007_0004.html
- (b)(3) exception: employment "at an annual salary which equals, or which may reasonably be expected to equal twenty thousand dollars ($20,000), or more." https://www.ksrevisor.org/statutes/chapters/ch50/050_007_0004.html

### Maryland
- Com. Law 14-1203(a)(5): "Records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of crime which, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven years". https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-1203&enactments=false
- (b)(3) exception: employment "at an annual salary which equals, or which may reasonably be expected to equal, $75,000 or more." https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-1203&enactments=false

### Massachusetts
- G.L. c. 93, s. 52(a)(5): "Records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of crime which, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven years." (a)(7) also bars "Eviction records sealed pursuant to section 16 of chapter 239." https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93/Section52
- 52(b) exceptions: only credit and life insurance of $50,000 or more. No employment salary exception in this section. https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93/Section52

### Montana
- MCA 31-3-112(5): "records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of crime which, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than 7 years". The section as read lists no exceptions. https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0310/chapter_0030/part_0010/section_0120/0310-0030-0010-0120.html

### New Hampshire
- RSA 359-B:5, I(e): "Records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of crime which, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than 7 years." https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXI/359-B/359-B-5.htm
- II(c) exception: employment "at an annual salary which equals, or which may reasonably be expected to equal, $20,000 or more." https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXI/359-B/359-B-5.htm

### New Mexico
- NMSA 56-3-6(A)(5): a credit bureau may report "arrests and indictments pending trial, or convictions of crimes, for not longer than seven years from date of release or parole. Such items shall no longer be reported if at any time it is learned that after a conviction a full pardon has been granted, or after an arrest or indictment a conviction did not result". No salary exception in the section. https://codes.findlaw.com/nm/chapter-56-commercial-instruments-and-transactions/nm-st-sect-56-3-6/
- Read on FindLaw ("Current as of January 01, 2024"); official NM source NOT READ.

### New York
- GBL 380-j(a): no consumer reporting agency shall report or maintain information "(1) relative to an arrest or a criminal charge unless there has been a criminal conviction for such offense, or unless such charges are still pending". https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/380-J
- 380-j(f)(1)(v): no report containing "records of conviction of crime which, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven years". https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/380-J
- 380-j(f)(2)(iii): "The provisions of this subdivision shall not apply to: ... the employment of any individual at an annual salary which equals, or which may reasonably be expected to equal twenty-five thousand dollars, or more." (The exception is in subdivision (f); the arrest rule is in subdivision (a).) https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/380-J
- 380-j(e): agencies "shall maintain reasonable procedures designed to assure maximum possible accuracy". Page showed most recent revision from 2026-06-19. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/380-J

### Washington
- RCW 19.182.040(1): may not report "(e) Records of arrest, indictment, or conviction of an adult for a crime that, from date of disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven years;" and "(f) Juvenile records, as defined in RCW 13.50.010 (1)(f), when the subject of the records is twenty-one years of age or older at the time of the report". https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.182.040
- (2)(c) exception (applies to (1)(a)-(e) and (h)): employment "at an annual salary that equals, or that may reasonably be expected to equal, twenty thousand dollars or more." https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.182.040

Note across states: these laws count 7 years "from date of disposition, release, or parole" (NM: "from date of release or parole"), not from the date of arrest.

## 4. FBI and state fingerprint checks vs. commercial checks
- 28 CFR 50.12(a): the FBI may exchange identification records with banks and, "if authorized by state statute and approved by the Director of the FBI ... with officials of state and local governments for purposes of employment and licensing, pursuant to section 201 of Public Law 92-544", plus certain securities, futures and nuclear-plant uses "and in other instances as authorized by federal law." https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/50.12
- 50.12(b): "effective September 6, 1990, the FBI Criminal Justice Information Services (CJIS) Division has made all data on identification records available for such purposes." https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/50.12
- 50.12(b): officials must tell people their fingerprints will be used to check FBI records; "The officials making the determination of suitability for licensing or employment shall provide the applicants the opportunity to complete, or challenge the accuracy of, the information contained in the FBI identification record." They "should not deny the license or employment based on information in the record until the applicant has been afforded a reasonable time to correct or complete the record, or has declined to do so." Corrections go through 28 CFR 16.34. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/50.12
- No 7-year limit appears in 28 CFR 50.12. Whether FCRA 1681c applies to FBI/state-repository checks: not researched; the guide should not claim a legal rule, only that the FCRA limit is written for consumer reporting agencies and that 50.12 makes "all data" available.
- FBI Identity History Summary pages (fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/identity-history-summary-checks and its FAQ): NOT READ (bot check).

## 5. Employment vs. tenant vs. licensing
- Employment: employers using a background reporting company must tell you in writing in a standalone document and get your written permission; before deciding against you because of the report they must give you a copy and a "Summary of Rights"; after, they must give the company's contact details and your right to a free report within 60 days and to dispute. https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/employer-background-checks-your-rights
- FTC: "Laws in your city or state might impact if or when employers can ask about your criminal or credit history." https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/employer-background-checks-your-rights
- Tenant: reports may include "criminal records, including arrest, charge, and conviction records"; same 7-year rule, no time limit for convictions; adverse action notice and free copy within 60 days; company must investigate a dispute within 30 days. https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights
- Licensing: FBI fingerprint checks for state "employment and licensing" happen when authorized by state statute (28 CFR 50.12(a)). https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/50.12

## 6. Expunged and sealed records
- 15 U.S.C. 1681e(b): "Whenever a consumer reporting agency prepares a consumer report it shall follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of the information concerning the individual about whom the report relates." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681e
- CFPB: "If any of your criminal records was expunged or removed from public records, they should not appear on your report." https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/
- FTC lists "criminal record or eviction information that was sealed or expunged" as a common error. https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights
- CFPB advisory opinion (Jan. 23, 2024): a company is not using reasonable procedures unless it has procedures so that "it does not report information that is duplicative or that has been expunged, sealed, or otherwise legally restricted from public access in a manner that would prevent the user from obtaining it directly from the government entities that maintain the records" and includes "any existing disposition information if it reports arrests, criminal charges, eviction proceedings, or other court filings." https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-01-23/html/2024-00788.htm
- Withdrawn May 12, 2025: the list of withdrawn guidance includes "Fair Credit Reporting; Background Screening, 89 FR 4171 (Jan. 23, 2024)." "Such withdrawal is not necessarily final. The Bureau intends to continue reviewing all guidance documents to determine whether they should ultimately be retained." https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-05-12/html/2025-08286.htm
- State law examples: Colorado 5-18-109(1)(e.5) bars reporting "Sealed records, expunged records, and records that did not result in a conviction" (FindLaw link above). California Gov. Code 12952(a)(3)(C) bars employers with 5+ employees from considering convictions "that have been sealed, dismissed, expunged, or statutorily eradicated pursuant to law" during a conviction history check. https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-12952/

## 7. Disputes
- 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A): if the consumer disputes an item, "the agency shall, free of charge, conduct a reasonable reinvestigation ... and record the current status of the disputed information, or delete the item from the file ... before the end of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the agency receives the notice of the dispute". https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i
- (a)(1)(B): may be extended "for not more than 15 additional days if the consumer reporting agency receives information from the consumer during that 30-day period that is relevant to the reinvestigation." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i
- CFPB steps: contact both the company that created the check and whoever reported the wrong information; describe the error in writing; include copies of supporting documents; "For errors about evictions or criminal records, you can also contact the court." Complaints can be submitted to the CFPB; free legal aid may help. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/
- FTC: after a correction, ask the company to send the corrected report to the employer; report problems at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/employer-background-checks-your-rights

## 8. Ban-the-box / fair chance (brief)
- Federal: 5 U.S.C. 9202(a): a federal agency employee "may not request ... that an applicant for an appointment to a position in the civil service disclose criminal history record information regarding the applicant before the appointing authority extends a conditional offer", with exceptions where required by law and for certain positions. Took effect 2 years after Dec. 20, 2019. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/9202
- California Gov. Code 12952: employers with 5+ employees may not ask about conviction history before a conditional offer; must do an "individualized assessment"; applicant gets "at least five business days to respond" to a preliminary decision; exceptions where law requires a check. https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-12952/
