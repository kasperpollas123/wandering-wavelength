# Fact sheet: How long does expungement take? (waiting periods, processing time, automatic relief)

Researched 2026-11-20. Each fact comes from a page read on that date, with its URL after it. Quotes are verbatim; everything else is a close paraphrase. Where a page could not be read, it says so.

Two separate "how longs":
1. **Waiting period**: how long you must wait after the case or sentence before you are allowed to apply. Set by statute; varies by state and offense level.
2. **Processing time**: how long it takes once you file (prosecutor objection windows, hearing dates, agency update deadlines). Statutes set some deadlines; almost no state publishes an average start-to-finish processing time. Only two official processing estimates were found and read (Utah courts: automatic expungement "can take up to 120 days"; Utah BCI certificate "can take several months"). Florida's FDLE figure was seen only in a search snippet (see Florida) and is NOT READ.

---

## 1. Waiting periods before you can apply (petition-based)

### California (dismissal / set-aside, Penal Code 1203.4, 1203.4a)
- Misdemeanor with no probation granted: may apply "after the lapse of one year from the date of pronouncement of judgment" (1203.4a(a)). https://california.public.law/codes/ca_penal_code_section_1203.4a
- California courts' self-help page says "true expungement does not exist in California" and gives no processing times; it points to a questionnaire and legal help. https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/clean-your-record
- (Probation cases: relief after probation is completed, per PC 1203.4. Statute text for 1203.4 not re-read for this sheet; the site's state data cites https://california.public.law/codes/ca_penal_code_section_1203.4.)

### Texas (nondisclosure, Gov't Code ch. 411; expunction, CCP ch. 55A)
- Nondisclosure after a misdemeanor conviction (411.0735(d)): petition "on or after (1) the date of completion of the person's sentence, if the offense ... was a misdemeanor punishable by fine only; or (2) the second anniversary of the date of completion of the person's sentence" for other eligible misdemeanors. Only for people never previously convicted (other than fine-only traffic). https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Nondisclosure after deferred adjudication (411.0725(e)): on or after the discharge and dismissal for most misdemeanors; the second anniversary for misdemeanors under Penal Code chapters 20, 21, 22, 25, 42, 43 or 46; "the fifth anniversary of the discharge and dismissal" for a felony. https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Certain nonviolent misdemeanor deferred adjudications (411.072): the court "shall issue" the nondisclosure order itself when it discharges and dismisses, if requirements are met. https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Expunction of an arrest with no indictment/information (CCP 55A.052(a)): Class C: "at least 180 days have elapsed from the date of arrest"; Class B or A: "at least one year"; felony: "at least three years". Earlier if the prosecutor certifies the records aren't needed (a)(4). https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/CR/htm/CR.55a.htm

### Florida
- Court-ordered expunction (Fla. Stat. 943.0585(1)(h), 2026 Statutes): if adjudication was withheld, the person must have had the record sealed "for a minimum of 10 years" first. That 10-year requirement does not apply "if a plea was not entered or all charges ... were dismissed before trial" or there was an acquittal. http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0900-0999/0943/Sections/0943.0585.html
- FDLE "shall forward the order to expunge to the Federal Bureau of Investigation." No FDLE deadline stated. (same URL)
- NOT READ: FDLE seal/expunge pages (403 and "Page Not Found" on every URL tried, including /Seal-and-Expunge-Process/Frequently-Asked-Questions and /Certificate-of-Eligibility-Instructions). A search-engine summary said FDLE's site gives "typically 12 weeks" to process a certificate of eligibility and does not expedite; this was NOT verified on an FDLE page and must not be used as fact.

### New York (sealing; Clean Slate is automatic, see section 3)
- Automatic sealing periods (CPL 160.57(1)(b)): misdemeanor, 3 years from "the defendant's release from incarceration or the imposition of sentence if there was no sentence of incarceration"; felony, 8 years from "the date the defendant was last released from incarceration". https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CPL/160.57

### Pennsylvania (petition for limited access, 18 Pa.C.S. 9122.1)
- Petition after being "free from conviction for a period of seven years for an offense punishable by one or more years in prison" (a); 10 years for the (a.1) category. https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/18/00.091.022.001..HTM

### Illinois (sealing, 20 ILCS 2630/5.2(c)(3))
- Release without charging, acquittal, dismissal: "may be sealed at any time." https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=002026300K5.2
- Supervision, qualified probation, convictions with conditional discharge or probation completed without revocation, and misdemeanor convictions: "may be sealed 2 years after the termination of petitioner's last sentence". (same URL)
- Other eligible felony convictions: "may be sealed 3 years after the termination of the petitioner's last sentence"; can be immediately on termination if the person earned a diploma/degree/GED etc. during the sentence. (same URL; source note lists amendments through P.A. 104-852, eff. 8-21-26)

### Ohio (R.C. 2953.32)
- Sealing: one or two third-degree felonies, "three years after the offender's final discharge"; fourth/fifth-degree felonies or misdemeanors, "one year after the offender's final discharge"; minor misdemeanor, "six months". https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2953.32
- Expungement: misdemeanor one year after final discharge; minor misdemeanor six months; felony "ten years after the time specified in division (B)(1)(a)" (i.e., 10 years after becoming eligible for sealing). (same URL)

### Georgia (record restriction, O.C.G.A. 35-3-37) - from 2020 bill text only
- SB 288 (2020, "As Passed") sets misdemeanor-conviction restriction for people who completed the sentence and have "not been convicted of any crime in any jurisdiction for at least four years prior to filing a petition" (excluding nonserious traffic offenses), plus no arrest for a period. Read via pdftotext; the PDF mixes struck and inserted text, so exact current wording NOT VERIFIED against the current code (Justia blocked by bot check). https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2020-signed-legislation/sb-288/download
- GBI: for arrests after July 1, 2013 "there is no application process"; contact the prosecutor. Before that date, apply at the arresting agency. No timeframes given. https://gbi.georgia.gov/node/2031
- Georgia courts page: "The prosecutor may approve the restriction at the time of sentencing." No timeframes. https://georgiacourts.gov/?p=18082

### North Carolina (expunction, G.S. 15A-145.5(c))
- One nonviolent misdemeanor: "three years after the date of the conviction" or after any active sentence/probation/post-release supervision, whichever is later. More than one misdemeanor: seven years after the last conviction (or sentence served). One nonviolent felony: 10 years (15 for G.S. 14-54(a)). Two or three felonies: "20 years after the date of the most recent conviction". https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_15A/GS_15A-145.5.html

### Michigan (set-aside, MCL 780.621d)
- Misdemeanors (not serious misdemeanor, first OWI or assaultive): "3 or more years" after the last of sentencing, imprisonment or probation (sub. 3). https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-780-621d
- One or more serious misdemeanors, one first OWI, or one felony: "5 or more years" (sub. 2). More than one felony: "7 or more years" (sub. 1). (same URL)
- If denied, wait 3 years to refile unless the court sets an earlier date (sub. 5). (same URL)

### New Jersey
- "In most cases, you must wait five years after your sentence is served for indictable and disorderly persons offenses"; ordinance offenses two years; "Sometimes, you can file for expungement after four years." Clean Slate: "At least ten years must have passed since your last conviction." https://www.njcourts.gov/self-help/expunge-record

### Virginia (petition sealing, Va. Code 19.2-392.12(F)(2))
- No conviction of a qualifying offense during "Seven years for any misdemeanor offense" and "Ten years for any felony offense", counted from the later of dismissal, conviction, release or violation date. https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title19.2/chapter23.2/section19.2-392.12/

### Washington (vacating)
- Misdemeanor/gross misdemeanor (RCW 9.96.060): "less than three years have passed since the later of the applicant's release from supervision or probation", release from confinement, or sentencing bars vacating; domestic violence: five years after completing the original sentence conditions. https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.96.060
- Felonies (RCW 9.94A.640): class B, ten years; class C, five years, from the later of release from community custody, release from confinement, or sentencing. https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.94A.640

### Arizona (sealing, A.R.S. 13-911(E))
- After completing the sentence: "Two years for a class 2 or 3 misdemeanor"; "Three years for a class 1 misdemeanor"; "Five years for a class 4, 5 or 6 felony"; "Ten years for a class 2 or 3 felony." After a denial, "a person may not file a new petition until three years after the date of the denial." https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00911.htm

### Massachusetts (sealing by request to the Commissioner of Probation, G.L. c. 276 s. 100A)
- Misdemeanor record: "not less than 3 years before the request"; felony: "not less than 7 years before the request". No stated time for the commissioner to act. https://malegislature.gov/laws/generallaws/partiv/titleii/chapter276/section100a

### Colorado (sealing convictions by petition)
- Colorado Judicial "Sealing Criminal Records - January 2026" clinic slides, eligibility time frames after final disposition: petty and drug petty offenses "1 year or more"; class 2 and 3 misdemeanors and any drug misdemeanor "2 Years or more"; class 4-6 felonies, level 2-4 drug felonies and class 1 misdemeanors "3 years or more"; all other offenses "5 years or more". A motion can be filed "once every twelve-month period." DUIs/DWAIs, class 1-3 felonies and crimes of violence are listed as not eligible. https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/Sealing%20Criminal%20Records%20-%20January%202026.pdf

### Minnesota (petition, Minn. Stat. 609A.02 subd. 3)
- Misdemeanor: no new conviction "for at least two years since discharge of the sentence"; gross misdemeanor: three years; listed felonies: four years (some deemed-misdemeanor felonies five). https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/609A.02

### Utah (petition)
- Utah courts: 10 years for certain DUI-related offenses; 7 years for other felonies; 5 years for class A misdemeanors and felony drug possession; 4 years for class B; 3 years for other misdemeanors and infractions, from the latest of case completion, release, or end of probation/parole. https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/criminal-justice/expunge.html

### Delaware (mandatory expungement, 11 Del. C. 4373)
- Misdemeanors in one case where "5 years have passed since the date of conviction" and no disqualifying convictions; violation conviction after 3 years; cases terminated in favor of the accused. https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c043/sc07/index.html

## 2. Processing time after filing (statutory deadlines read)

### Prosecutor objection windows and hearing schedules
- Ohio: "The court shall hold the hearing not less than forty-five days and not more than ninety days from the date of the filing of the application." Prosecutor notified "not less than sixty days prior to the hearing"; objections "not later than thirty days prior to the date set for the hearing." https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2953.32
- New Jersey: "The Superior Court judge assigned to your case will usually schedule a hearing between 35 and 60 days after he or she gets your petition." If no law enforcement objects, "the court may order the expungement of your records without a hearing." (NJ Courts expungement kit, "Kit updated 06/2020", CN 10557) https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/forms/10557_expunge_kit.pdf
- Pennsylvania: "The court shall provide notice of the filing of a petition ... to the district attorney within 10 days." "Within 30 days of receipt of notice, the district attorney may file objections." With no objection the court "may grant the petition without further hearing." https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/18/00.091.022.001..HTM
- Illinois: "Objections to a petition to expunge or seal must be filed within 60 days of the date of service of the petition." Unless an objection is filed within 60 days, "the court shall enter an order granting or denying the petition." https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=002026300K5.2
- North Carolina: the DA "shall then have 30 days to file an objection"; the court may give the DA an additional 30 days for good cause. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_15A/GS_15A-145.5.html
- Virginia: the prosecutor may object or give notice of no objection "within 30 days after it is delivered to him or received in the mail"; the court "may conduct a hearing"; it may seal without a hearing if the prosecutor does not object and stipulates eligibility. https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title19.2/chapter23.2/section19.2-392.12/
- Arizona: the court "may not grant or deny a petition to seal a person's case records until sixty calendar days" after receiving it, unless the prosecutor and victims don't object; hearing not automatic. https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00911.htm
- Minnesota: "A hearing on the petition shall be held no sooner than 60 days after service of the petition" (609A.03 subd. 4). An order granting relief "shall be stayed automatically for 60 days after the order is filed" (subd. 9). https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/609A.03
- Utah: prosecutor has 35 days to object; victim 60 days; court must wait 60 days from filing before ruling; BCI certificate of eligibility: "It can take several months for BCI to process your request"; certificate valid 180 days. https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/criminal-justice/expunge.html
- Texas expunction: hearing "not earlier than the 30th day following the date the petition is filed" (CCP 55A.254(a)). https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/CR/htm/CR.55a.htm
- Georgia (2020 SB 288 text): for petitions, "If a hearing is requested, such hearing shall be held within 90 days of the filing of the petition." For pre-July 2013 arrests: arresting agency sends request to prosecutor within 30 days; prosecutor decides within 90 days. https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2020-signed-legislation/sb-288/download
- Colorado: non-conviction motions - court sets "a return date no later than thirty-five days after the motion is filed"; arrest-no-charges petitions - a hearing "will be set at least 35 days out" and "If no objections are filed at least 7 days prior to the hearing date, the court shall vacate the hearing and grant your request"; municipal conviction - if the prosecution objects "a hearing will be set within 42 days." https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/Sealing%20Criminal%20Records%20-%20January%202026.pdf
- Michigan: the court "shall not act upon the application until the department of state police reports" its fingerprint check (including FBI comparison) to the court; copy served on the attorney general and prosecutor with "an opportunity ... to contest" (no day count). $50 fee to state police. https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-780-621d

### Agency deadlines after an order
- Illinois: records expunged "by the arresting agency, the Illinois State Police, and any other agency as ordered by the court, within 60 days of the date of service of the order"; sealing: parties "must fully comply with the terms of the order within 60 days of service of the order"; ISP sends the petitioner written notice of compliance within 60 days. https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=002026300K5.2
- Texas nondisclosure (Gov't Code 411.075): clerk sends order to DPS "Not later than the 15th business day after the date an order ... is issued"; DPS seals "Not later than 10 business days after receipt" and sends the order to agencies, "central federal depositories" and private entities that buy records; agencies seal "Not later than 30 business days after receipt". https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Texas private companies (411.0851(a)): a private entity that compiles and sells criminal history "shall destroy and may not disseminate" information once it "has received notice" of an expunction or nondisclosure order. Non-FCRA entities buying from the state may disseminate only information obtained or updated "within the 90-day period preceding the date of dissemination" (b). https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
- Texas expunction: clerk must send the order, but statute sets no time limit; no deadline for DPS/agencies (55A.351-353). https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/CR/htm/CR.55a.htm
- Georgia (2020 SB 288 text): GCIC notifies the arresting agency within 30 days of restriction, and the agency "shall, within 30 days, restrict access". https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2020-signed-legislation/sb-288/download
- New Jersey: "After a judge signs your expungement order, the order will be transmitted to law enforcement agencies" (njcourts.gov). The 2020 kit instead tells petitioners to mail the signed order by certified mail to the listed agencies themselves. No deadlines stated. https://www.njcourts.gov/self-help/expunge-record ; https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/forms/10557_expunge_kit.pdf
- No stated deadline in: Ohio (2953.32 - court forwards to BCI; others "shall destroy, delete, and erase" once notified), North Carolina (15A-145.5), Virginia (19.2-392.12 - State Police seals "upon receipt"), Arizona (13-911), Minnesota petitions (609A.03), Florida (943.0585), Massachusetts (276 s.100A). URLs above.
- Utah: no agency timeline; for traffic and cannabis petitions "your records will be expunged shortly after the court signs the order." https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/criminal-justice/expunge.html

### FBI record
- 28 CFR 16.34: to change an FBI identification record, apply to the agency that contributed the information, or send the challenge to the FBI CJIS Division, which forwards it to the contributing agency; the FBI makes changes only after an official communication from that agency. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/16.34
- Florida 943.0585: FDLE "shall forward the order to expunge to the Federal Bureau of Investigation." Texas 411.075(b): DPS sends nondisclosure orders to "central federal depositories of criminal records". Michigan 780.621d(8): state police forward fingerprints to the FBI as part of the application check. URLs above.
- No FBI page stating how long its update takes was read (fbi.gov FAQ returned 403).

### Private background check companies (federal law)
- FCRA 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A): after a consumer disputes an item, the agency must reinvestigate "before the end of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the agency receives the notice of the dispute"; extendable "for not more than 15 additional days" if the consumer sends relevant information during the period (a)(1)(B); inaccurate or unverifiable items must be "promptly delete[d]" (a)(5)(A); written results "not later than 5 business days after the completion of the reinvestigation" (a)(6)(A). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i

## 3. Automatic / Clean Slate timelines (verified)

- **Pennsylvania** (18 Pa.C.S. 9122.2): limited access automatically for non-convictions; summary convictions "when five years have elapsed since entry of the judgment of conviction"; certain misdemeanors "if a person has been free for seven years from conviction" (restitution paid); qualifying offenses after 10 years. "On a monthly basis, the Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts shall transmit" eligible records to the State Police; State Police have 30 days to flag ineligible records; "Each court of common pleas shall issue monthly an order for limited access". Non-convictions sent within 30 days after disposition and restitution payment. https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/18/00.091.022.002..HTM
- **Michigan** (MCL 780.621g): misdemeanors set aside without application when "7 years have passed from the imposition of the sentence"; felonies when "Ten years have passed" from the later of sentencing or release from imprisonment; no new conviction and no pending charges; lifetime cap of 2 felonies and 4 misdemeanors; excludes assaultive crimes, serious misdemeanors, crimes of dishonesty and others. Section added by 2020 PA 193; "The implementation of the section is subject to appropriation." No fixed processing schedule for the state police in the text. First OWI cannot be set aside automatically (780.621c(3)). https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-780-621g ; https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-780-621c
  - NOT READ: Michigan State Police Clean Slate page (403).
- **New Jersey**: no automatic clearing of convictions found on official pages read; "Clean Slate" in NJ is an application after 10 years (njcourts.gov). Not verified whether any automated system is running. https://www.njcourts.gov/self-help/expunge-record
- **Utah**: courts automatically expunge class C misdemeanors and infractions after 5 years, class B after 6, class A drug possession after 7 (with fines, fees and restitution paid); dismissals with prejudice "within 180 days of the dismissal"; acquittals "within 60 days of acquittal." "The process can take up to 120 days." https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/criminal-justice/expunge.html
- **California** (PC 1203.425): DOJ reviews records "on a monthly basis" (subject to Budget Act appropriation). Misdemeanor/infraction without probation: eligible when the person appears to have completed the sentence and "at least one calendar year has elapsed since the date of judgment"; probation cases: completed probation without revocation; certain felonies: "a period of four years has elapsed since the date on which the defendant completed probation or supervision." Court "shall update its records". The statute text summary referenced July 1, 2022 (DOJ notices begin), January 1, 2023 and October 1, 2024 dates; how each applies was not fully clear from the read. https://california.public.law/codes/ca_penal_code_section_1203.425
- **Connecticut**: official portal says programs "will automatically erase certain types of convictions beginning in 2023" while the timeline graphic says "Automatic Erasures begins January 1, 2024"; "Clean Slate erasures have now resumed after months of delay"; about 50,000 people have had records partially or completely erased; "in coming weeks we expect to have erasures for more than 100,000 people" (no date on the update). Waiting periods (7/10 years) NOT VERIFIED here: cga.ct.gov chapter 961a returned 503. https://portal.ct.gov/cleanslate
- **Delaware** (11 Del. C. 4373A): cases eligible for mandatory expungement are eligible for automatic expungement; "Beginning August 1, 2024, on a monthly basis," the SBI identifies eligible cases. People can still petition if automatic expungement has not happened (4373A(d)). https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c043/sc07/index.html
- **Oklahoma**: OSBI: Clean Slate "allows for the expungement of certain eligible criminal history records and does not require a court petition." "On November 1, 2026, the OSBI launched the expedited clean slate portal." Only records with a final disposition qualify. No waiting periods or processing times on the page. Contact cleanslate@osbi.ok.gov, 405-879-2641. https://oklahoma.gov/osbi/cleanslate.html
- **Virginia** (19.2-392.6): listed misdemeanors (e.g., 18.2-96, 18.2-103, 18.2-119, 18.2-120, 18.2-134, former 18.2-248.1, 18.2-415) automatically sealed "if seven years have passed since the date of the conviction" with no reportable conviction in that time. Page shows versions effective until and after July 1, 2027. No State Police timing stated. https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title19.2/chapter23.2/section19.2-392.6/
- **Colorado** (C.R.S. 13-3-117 as amended by SB22-099): state court administrator lists eligible convictions: civil infraction after four years; petty offense or misdemeanor "at least seven years"; eligible felony "at least ten years" after final disposition or release from supervision, whichever is later; no intervening conviction. List compiled "ON A QUARTERLY BASIS"; felonies included "BEGINNING ON JULY 1, 2025." District attorneys "MAY, WITHIN FORTY-FIVE DAYS, OBJECT"; courts "SHALL ENTER SEALING ORDERS ... WITHIN FOURTEEN DAYS AFTER RECEIPT OF THE AMENDED LIST"; orders sent electronically to CBI; a website lets people check whether their conviction was sealed. https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2022a_099_signed.pdf
  - Colorado non-convictions (acquittal, full dismissal, completed diversion or deferred judgment): "the sealing process is intended to be automatic." https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/Sealing%20Criminal%20Records%20-%20January%202026.pdf
- **Minnesota** (609A.015): automatic sealing after discharge: misdemeanor and petty misdemeanor two years; gross misdemeanor three years; listed felonies five years (152.025 felony four years); diversion/stay of adjudication one year. BCA makes "an initial determination of eligibility within 30 days of the end of the applicable waiting period"; notice to courts "may be made in real time or in the form of a monthly report"; "Records shall be sealed 60 days after notice is sent to the judicial branch." Applies to records in the BCA system as of January 1, 2025. https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/609A.015
- **New York** (CPL 160.57): automatic sealing after 3 years (misdemeanor) / 8 years (felony); excludes sex offenses, most class A felonies, people still on probation/parole or with pending charges. For older convictions, OCA "shall ensure sealing of such convictions is complete no later than three years after such effective date." If a conviction should have been sealed and a form is submitted, it "shall be sealed ... within thirty days of the receipt of such form." Eligibility rechecked "no less than quarterly" when blocked by pending charges or supervision. Effective date not printed on the page; NY courts Clean Slate page (nycourts.gov/clean-slate) returned 403, so the exact deadline date is NOT VERIFIED. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CPL/160.57
- **North Carolina** (15A-146(a4)): dismissed and not-guilty charges disposed on or after December 1, 2021 are expunged "by operation of law not less than 180 days and not more than 210 days after the date of final disposition" (not felony charges dismissed under a plea agreement). https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_15A/GS_15A-146.html
- **Texas**: certain nonviolent misdemeanor deferred adjudications get a nondisclosure order issued by the court at discharge (411.072). https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/GV/htm/GV.411.htm
