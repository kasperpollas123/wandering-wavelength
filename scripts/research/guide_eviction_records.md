# Removing an eviction from your record — fact sheet

Research date: November 20, 2026. Every bullet is taken from the source linked after it, opened on that date. This is general information, not legal advice.

## 1. What an eviction record is and where it shows up

- An eviction case creates court records: the complaint and other filings, proof of service, the court's findings and orders, and other papers. State sealing laws define the "court file" in these terms. [https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-040.html]
- A filing is not the same as a judgment. A case can be withdrawn, dismissed, settled, or decided for the tenant, and still leave a court record. Several state laws below exist specifically to hide cases that did not end in a judgment for the landlord. [https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=grp&section=8-503&enactments=false]
- Tenant background checks (tenant screening reports) "can include eviction records, criminal records, civil judgments, and credit history". [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]
- The CFPB lists things to check about an eviction on a screening report: the final outcome should be shown (for example "dismissed"); one eviction should not be listed several times; sealed or expunged evictions should not appear. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]
- The FTC tells renters to check housing court records before applying, and says that if the records are wrong or incomplete they can ask the court to correct them. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]
- In 2023 the FTC and CFPB alleged that a large screening company reported one eviction case as several events, reported dismissed cases as judgments for the landlord, labelled the landlord's claim as a "Judgment Amount", and failed to keep sealed eviction records out of reports. The company agreed to pay $15 million. [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/10/ftc-cfpb-settlement-require-trans-union-pay-15-million-over-charges-it-failed-ensure-accuracy-tenant]

## 2. The federal 7-year limit (Fair Credit Reporting Act)

- A consumer reporting agency may not report "Civil suits, civil judgments, and records of arrest that, from date of entry, antedate the report by more than seven years or until the governing statute of limitations has expired, whichever is the longer period." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c]
- It also may not report "Any other adverse item of information, other than records of convictions of crimes which antedates the report by more than seven years." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c]
- The CFPB puts it this way for renters: "Federal law generally bars evictions from appearing after seven years", and a civil judgment can generally be reported for seven years after it is issued or until the statute of limitations on the debt expires, whichever is longer. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]
- The FTC says the 7-year limit covers "most civil lawsuits and judgments (including housing court cases)". [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]
- The limit does not apply to a report used in connection with a credit transaction of $150,000 or more, life insurance of $150,000 or more, or employment at an annual salary of $75,000 or more. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c]
- The limit is a limit on consumer reporting agencies. It does not remove the case from the court's own records; that depends on state law (section 3). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c]

## 3. States with laws to seal, mask, limit access to or expunge eviction records

**This list may not be complete.** It contains only the laws I could open and read. States not listed were either not checked or could not be confirmed (see "Could not confirm"). Laws change; each entry gives the year shown in the source.

- **Arizona** — Court seals the eviction case when it is dismissed before judgment or decided for the tenant; also on a written agreement of landlord and tenant to set aside the eviction order and seal. Sealed cases may not be sold or released in bulk. *Automatic or by request:* Court order on dismissal or tenant win (no motion mentioned in the text); or by joint stipulation. *Year:* Year not shown on the statute page. [https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/01379.htm]
- **California** — Clerk limits access to eviction (unlawful detainer) records to the parties and a few others. The record opens to the public only if the landlord prevails within 60 days of filing, or by court order after a later trial judgment for the landlord. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic. *Year:* Bill AB 2819 enrolled in 2016. [https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/15-16/bill/asm/ab_2801-2850/ab_2819_bill_20160817_enrolled.htm]
- **Colorado** — Eviction court records are "suppressed" (not publicly available) while the case is open; they become public if the court grants the landlord possession, unless the parties agree otherwise. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic. *Year:* Signed March 20, 2020; effective December 1, 2020. [https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb20-1009]
- **Connecticut** — Judicial Department must remove the case from its website within 30 days after withdrawal, dismissal or nonsuit, or a judgment for the tenant. Removed records cannot be sold in bulk or used for commercial purposes such as consumer reports or tenant screening. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic. *Year:* Public Act 23-207, effective July 1, 2024. [https://www.cga.ct.gov/2023/ACT/PA/PDF/2023PA-00207-R00SB-00998-PA.PDF]
- **District of Columbia** — Court seals eviction records 30 days after the case ends if there was no judgment for possession for the landlord, and 3 years after if there was. A tenant can also ask for sealing at any time on listed grounds. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic, plus by motion. *Year:* D.C. Law 24-115, effective May 18, 2022. [https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/42-3505.09]
- **Idaho** — Records are shielded from public disclosure when the whole case was dismissed, no appeal is pending, and either 3 years have passed since filing or the parties file an agreement. Applies to cases filed on or after January 1, 2025. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic ("without the filing of a petition"); a separate petition to seal is also possible. *Year:* 2024. [https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title6/T6CH3/SECT6-303A/]
- **Illinois** — Court may seal a case that was "sufficiently without a basis in fact or law" where sealing is clearly in the interests of justice; must seal certain cases linked to foreclosure and cases dismissed under section 9-106. *Automatic or by request:* Discretionary and mandatory parts; the section does not say whether a motion is needed. *Year:* Current text effective January 1, 2026 (P.A. 104-317). [https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/073500050K9-121.htm]
- **Indiana** — Court must order the eviction record sealed when the case is dismissed (and in other listed situations). Since July 1, 2025 the court acts "on its own motion"; before that the tenant had to ask. A news report describes the other situations as a judgment for the tenant or a reversal on appeal. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic since July 1, 2025. *Year:* 2022 version; amended effective July 1, 2025. [https://caseclips.courts.in.gov/2026/03/09/jayla-anderson-v-advantix-development-corporation-no-25a-ev-1738-__-n-e-3d-__-ind-ct-app-mar-6-2026/] [https://stateline.org/2025/07/08/think-democrats-and-republicans-cant-work-together-on-sealing-eviction-records-they-do/]
- **Maryland** — In failure-to-pay-rent cases, the District Court shields the records within 60 days after the case ends if there was no judgment of possession. If there was one, the tenant can ask for shielding after redeeming and waiting 12 months, or for good cause. Does not apply to cases started before October 1, 2024. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic for cases without a judgment of possession; by motion otherwise. *Year:* Chapter 347 of 2024 (approved April 25, 2024). [https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=grp&section=8-503&enactments=false] [https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2024RS/chapters_noln/Ch_347_sb0019T.pdf]
- **Massachusetts** — Tenant may petition to seal: no-fault evictions at any time after the case ends; dismissed cases or tenant wins at any time; non-payment cases after 4 years; fault cases after 7 years (with conditions). Consumer reporting agencies must remove sealed records within 30 days. A person with a sealed record may answer "no record". *Automatic or by request:* By petition. *Year:* In effect from May 5, 2025 (date given by the Attorney General). [https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIII/TitleIII/Chapter239/Section16] [https://www.mass.gov/info-details/eviction-sealing]
- **Minnesota** — Court must expunge an eviction case when the tenant won, the case was dismissed, the parties agree, or 3 years have passed since the eviction was ordered, among other grounds. Court may also expunge where "clearly in the interests of justice". *Automatic or by request:* Mostly automatic ("without motion"); some grounds need the tenant's motion. *Year:* Section dates from 1999; amended 2023 and 2024. [https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/484.014]
- **Nevada** — In summary eviction cases the file is sealed automatically when the case is dismissed, 10 judicial days after it is denied, or 31 days after the tenant's affidavit if the landlord does not follow up. The court may also seal by agreement of the parties or on the tenant's motion. A sealed case is "deemed never to have occurred". *Automatic or by request:* Automatic, plus by motion or stipulation. *Year:* Added 2017; amended 2019 and 2021. [https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-040.html]
- **North Dakota** — A person evicted for non-payment or damage, who has resolved all claims, may move to seal the records 7 years after the eviction order was satisfied if not evicted again in that time. A domestic violence victim evicted because of an incident may move to seal once the assailant is convicted or a protective order is issued. *Automatic or by request:* By motion. *Year:* Senate Bill 2238, 2025 session. [https://ndlegis.gov/assembly/69-2025/regular/documents/25-0519-03000.pdf]
- **Oregon** — (1) Tenant may move to set aside the judgment and seal the records: judgment against the tenant after 5 years if any money award is satisfied; stipulated judgment once its terms are met; or judgment or dismissal in the tenant's favour. (2) Each year courts must themselves set aside and seal eligible residential cases: landlord judgments after 5 years, dismissals and tenant wins after 12 months, if there is no unpaid money award. *Automatic or by request:* By motion (ORS 105.163) and automatic annual sealing (ORS 105.164). *Year:* 105.163: 2019; 105.164: 2023, amended 2025. [https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors105.html]
- **Rhode Island** — Court must seal on a party's motion where the case was dismissed, a stipulation was satisfied, any money judgment was paid in full, or the case was dismissed for lack of prosecution after 5 years. The motion can be filed 30 days after the appeal period ends; one request every 5 years. *Automatic or by request:* By motion. *Year:* P.L. 2023, ch. 362, effective January 1, 2024. [https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE34/34-18/34-18-60.htm]
- **South Carolina** — Eviction filings — including cases that ended in an eviction order, a settlement, or later payment — must be removed from the public index and publicly accessible files 7 years after final disposition or filing, if no further filing has been recorded. *Automatic or by request:* Appears automatic (no request process in the text). *Year:* Act No. 214 of 2026, signed May 19, 2026; effective January 1, 2027 — not yet in force on the research date. [https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/4270.htm]
- **Utah** — Court expunges automatically when the whole case was dismissed, no appeal is pending and 3 years have passed since filing, or when landlord and tenant file an agreement. A tenant can also petition in non-payment and lease-expiry cases once any judgment is satisfied; if the landlord objects within 60 days the court must deny. Tenant screening agencies may not share an expunged case. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic, plus by petition. *Year:* Utah Code Title 78B, Chapter 6, Part 8a (year not shown on the court page). [https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/housing/landlord/expunge.html]
- **Virginia** — When an unlawful detainer case in general district court is dismissed (after 30 days) or nonsuited (after 6 months) and no order of possession was entered, the court orders the records expunged "without further petition or hearing". For a judgment for the tenant, and for cases started before July 1, 2024, the tenant petitions on a court form. *Automatic or by request:* Automatic for dismissals and nonsuits; by petition otherwise. *Year:* 2020; amended 2024. [https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title8.01/chapter3/section8.01-130.01/]
- **Washington** — Court may issue an "order for limited dissemination" where the landlord's case lacked sufficient basis, the tenancy was reinstated, or for other good cause. A tenant screening provider must then not disclose the case or use it in a score. *Automatic or by request:* Court order (the section does not say how it is requested). *Year:* 2016. [https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.367]

Related law that is not a sealing law:

- **Wisconsin** — The statute sets minimum periods that eviction cases stay on the state's online court record site: at least 10 years where a writ of restitution was granted, and at least 2 years where the case was dismissed with no money judgment. It does not seal the court file. (2017 Act 317; amended 2019.) [https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/758/20]

## 4. Other routes that exist generally

- **Asking the court to set aside (vacate) a judgment.** Courts have procedures to cancel a judgment in limited circumstances. As one example, California's court system says a tenant can ask for a "set aside" only for reasons such as "mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect", within 6 months of the judgment, and only once. Rules and deadlines differ in every state. [https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/eviction-tenant/lose-case/ask-set-aside]
- **Set-aside linked to sealing.** In some states a set-aside leads directly to sealing: Nevada lets the court seal when landlord and tenant agree in writing to set aside the eviction order, or when the court finds the eviction should be set aside under its rules. Arizona seals on a written agreement to set aside the order. [https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-040.html] [https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/01379.htm]
- **Satisfying (paying) the judgment.** Paying a money judgment does not by itself remove the record, but several state laws make it a condition of relief: Rhode Island (money judgment "satisfied in full"), Oregon (money award satisfied), Utah (a Satisfaction of Judgment must be filed with the court), North Dakota (all claims resolved and the order satisfied). [https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE34/34-18/34-18-60.htm] [https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors105.html] [https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/housing/landlord/expunge.html] [https://ndlegis.gov/assembly/69-2025/regular/documents/25-0519-03000.pdf]
- **Recording that a judgment was paid.** In Massachusetts the party who obtained a non-payment judgment must file a notice of satisfaction within 14 days of being paid; if they do not, the person who paid can ask the court to treat it as satisfied, and either side may then petition to seal. [https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIII/TitleIII/Chapter239/Section16]
- **Agreement with the landlord.** Several laws allow sealing or expungement when both sides agree: Arizona, Idaho, Minnesota, Nevada and Utah (see the table). The FTC also notes a tenant can ask a former landlord, in writing, to send updated or correct information to the court. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]
- **Disputing errors with the screening company.** Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, when a consumer disputes an item, the agency must, free of charge, carry out a reasonable reinvestigation and record the current status of the item or delete it within 30 days of receiving the dispute; this can be extended by up to 15 days if the consumer sends more information during the 30 days. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- If the item is found to be inaccurate, incomplete or unverifiable, the agency must promptly delete or modify it. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i]
- How to dispute, according to the FTC: contact the background check company and describe each error; include copies of supporting documents, such as paperwork showing how the eviction case was resolved; tell the landlord about the dispute; the company must investigate within 30 days and give the results in writing; if the report is corrected, give the landlord the updated report or ask the company to send it. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]
- The CFPB adds: contact both the screening company and whoever supplied the wrong information; describe the error in writing; for eviction record errors you can also contact the court. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/]
- **Getting the report.** If a landlord turns you down, asks for a co-signer, or charges more because of a report, the landlord must give you the screening company's name, address and phone number, and you can get a free copy from that company if you ask within 60 days. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-my-rental-application-is-denied-because-of-a-tenant-screening-report-en-2105/]
- **Complaints.** Problems with tenant screening can be reported to the CFPB (consumerfinance.gov/complaint or (855) 411-2372) and to the FTC (ReportFraud.ftc.gov). [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-my-rental-application-is-denied-because-of-a-tenant-screening-report-en-2105/] [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-and-your-rights]

## Could not confirm

- **The list is not a 50-state survey.** I verified 19 jurisdictions (18 states and the District of Columbia) plus Wisconsin's display rule. States not listed were not individually checked. One 2024 national housing guide counted 12 jurisdictions and a November 2024 public-health fact sheet described 7; counts differ by date and definition, and neither is official. [https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/AG-2024/7-2_Eviction-Record-Sealing-and-Expungement.pdf] [https://www.networkforphl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Limiting-Public-Access-to-Eviction-Records.pdf]
- **Georgia.** A version of 2026 Senate Bill 406 on the legislature's site (a House Rules Committee substitute) would let a court, on motion and after a hearing, make dispossessory records unavailable to the public where the tenant won, the judgment was paid in full, or 7 years have passed, effective January 1, 2027. Search results say the bill was signed on May 12, 2026, but I could not open the enrolled Act or the official status page, so I cannot confirm this text became law. [https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/248227]
- **Texas.** A 2024 housing guide says Texas adopted eviction record sealing "through administrative means"; I found no official Texas source and could not confirm what applies now. [https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/AG-2024/7-2_Eviction-Record-Sealing-and-Expungement.pdf]
- **New York, New Jersey, Nebraska, Florida, Delaware and others.** Not confirmed either way; I did not find or open an official source for them.
- **Bills that were pending, not law, when last reported (July 2025):** Hawaii, Kansas, Oklahoma and Pennsylvania had eviction-sealing bills awaiting passage, and Nevada's governor vetoed a bill to expand automatic sealing in June 2025. I did not check what happened to those bills afterwards. (South Carolina was on that pending list and has since enacted its law, as shown in the table.) [https://stateline.org/2025/07/08/think-democrats-and-republicans-cant-work-together-on-sealing-eviction-records-they-do/]
- **California current text.** The official code page for Code of Civil Procedure 1161.2 would not load. The entry relies on the 2016 enrolled bill on the legislature's older site; later amendments (if any) were not checked.
- **Arizona and Utah years.** The official pages I opened do not show the year of enactment. Utah's own statute page would not load; the entry relies on the Utah Courts self-help page.
- **Indiana statute text.** The entry relies on an Indiana Court of Appeals summary (March 2026) that quotes the statute; the code section itself (IC 32-31-11-3) was not opened, so the full list of sealing grounds is not confirmed.
- **Massachusetts year of enactment.** The Attorney General's page gives May 5, 2025 as the date the notice rules apply; news reports say the law was part of a 2024 housing act, but I did not open the session law.
- **Illinois history.** The section says it "is operative on and after August 1, 2022" and was last amended effective January 1, 2026; I did not trace what the 2026 amendment changed or when the section was first enacted.
- **Colorado current code.** The entry relies on the legislature's summary of the 2020 bill, not the current code section.
- **Whether sealed court records always disappear from screening reports.** The laws above bind courts, and some also bind screening companies (Connecticut, Massachusetts, Utah, Washington). How quickly private databases are updated in practice is not something I could confirm.
- **Federal statute text source.** Read on Cornell's Legal Information Institute; the official House site would not load section pages on the research date.
