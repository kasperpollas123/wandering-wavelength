# New revenue angles for ExpungementAttorneys.org (researched 2026-11-20)

This file covers new ground only. Pay-per-call, record-clearing affiliates, FCRA referrals, Checkr, flat sponsorships, display ads and quiz routing are in MONEY_NOTES.md and the other files in this folder.

**How to read the sources.** "Own page" means I read the company's own page. "Network" means I read the listing on the affiliate network (FlexOffers, Awin, PartnerStack). "3P" means a third-party directory or blog, so treat it as **unverified**. FlexOffers listings carry a "last updated" date, but none of them says plainly whether the programme is still active. Check every rate in the network dashboard before you place a link.

## Notes by area

### 1. Legal marketplaces and prepaid plans
- The marketplaces (LegalMatch, Martindale-Avvo/Nolo/Lawyers.com, FindLaw, Justia) earn money by selling to lawyers, and none pays websites per lead. This confirms ppc.md.
- LegalShield is the only legal brand with a working publisher programme, but the fit is poor. Its listed coverage (wills, traffic tickets, civil defense, a 15-25% discount on other services) does not mention expungement. At $36-60 a month it also does not suit the audience. Its payout comes only from network or third-party listings.
- ARAG plan pages mention expungement, but ARAG sells only through brokers, employers and membership groups. I found no affiliate programme (https://araglegal.com/partners).

### 2. Grants and nonprofit status
- **Ad Grants needs the site's operator to be a 501(c)(3).** Fiscally sponsored projects without their own 501(c)(3) status are not eligible (Google support 1699858). The website policy also bars excessive third-party ads and sites that mainly push affiliate traffic. Even if a nonprofit existed, ~$10k a month of free ads would mean dropping most of the affiliate angles in this file. Sponsorships might survive only if they are limited and the site explains how the money funds its programmes.
- Getting 501(c)(3) status needs a US entity and an IRS filing: $275 on Form 1023-EZ if gross receipts are $50k or less and assets $250k or less, otherwise $600 on Form 1023. Once it is a nonprofit, the site can't be run for private profit.
- The big funders in this field (Arnold Ventures, Clean Slate Initiative) work by invitation only. Fast Forward ($25K+) is the one realistic open call, and only if the site became a nonprofit tech project; its next intake would be around August 2027 (inferred from the 2026 cycle).
- The 2016 DOL "National Clean Slate Clearinghouse" bid that shows up in searches is long closed.

### 3. Second-chance jobs and housing
- No fair-chance job board pays referral commissions. Honest Jobs earns from employers ($199 or $499 a month) and corrections contracts. Jails to Jobs is a nonprofit. 70MillionJobs looks defunct.
- General job-feed CPC exists, but Talroo needs 6 months of traffic history and the jobs aren't fair-chance filtered. Revisit only if the site has real traffic.
- Housing: the locator model depends on licensed referral fees, and Texas forbids paying unlicensed referrers.

### 4. Financial products
- Credit-builder CPAs exist on FlexOffers (Kikoff $33, Self $12, Chime $10.80). The rates are 10-22 months old and their status is unclear. The angle is legitimate on a "rebuilding after a record" page, with clear labels. Avoid credit-repair firms (see the Lexington Law/CFPB note in affiliates.md).
- Bail bonds: rejected (table row 14).

### 5. Records-adjacent paid services
- **Data-broker removal is the strongest new find.** Sealed or expunged records often stay on people-search sites, and Optery's free plan lets readers check at no cost. Optery's terms are on its own page (30% for 12 months, PartnerStack). DeleteMe publishes 30-40% of AOV. Incogni's business rate is third-party only ($30 per sale on FlexOffers; not verified).
- FBI Identity History Summary channelers don't run publisher affiliate programmes. Their partner programmes recruit fingerprinting locations. State record checks are sold by government agencies and have no affiliate programmes.
- Passport expediters pay (Fast Passport Center $60 per approved order, network listing), but only a small share of readers need one.
- Pardon "consultants" are non-lawyer services, and no programme was found. I didn't pursue them further because of the risk of misleading readers.

### 6. Paid listing tiers, using what other directories charge
- Verified prices: Super Lawyers $75, $174 or $400 a month (annual); Justia Connect Pro $19.99 a month. Justia's top placements ("Fixed Top Position, Only One Per Practice Area/Metro") and Martindale-Avvo's tiers are priced on request.
- For this site, an honest version would be a flat-fee "enhanced profile" (photo, longer bio, fees, languages, a contact form) labelled "Enhanced listing (paid)". It would not change sort order or carry any "verified/best" wording. Free claiming and corrections stay free. This adds to the statewide sponsorship idea and doesn't replace it. Ethics as in fcra.md: flat advertising fee, no recommendation, extra care in NJ, FL and MI. In NJ, the bar has also said lawyers should not advertise Super Lawyers-type ratings (3P report).
- Selling the dataset itself (firm lists) has little value, because the data is public and the buyers (legal marketers) already scrape it. I didn't pursue it.
