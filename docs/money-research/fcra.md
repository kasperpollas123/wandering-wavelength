# Referring FCRA background-check cases: what exists and what ethics allow

Researched 2026-11-20. "Read" means I fetched the page itself. "Snippet" means I saw it only in search-result text and could not open the page. I have not guessed any payout.

## Bottom line

- None of the FCRA plaintiff firms I checked publicly offers a paid referral or lead program for non-lawyer publishers. The one paid program I found pays when a lawsuit is filed. That ties payment to outcome, which is the risky kind of payment (see section 3).
- The realistic paid models are:
  - a flat or monthly advertising fee paid by a firm for a sponsored placement (Top Class Actions and ClassAction.org work this way), or
  - a per-lead fee paid through a general legal lead network. None of the networks I found lists FCRA or background-check leads as a category.
- The good unpaid way to refer readers: a neutral list of several firms plus the NACA directory, with no endorsement.

## 2. Lead networks and legal marketing companies

None of these lists FCRA, credit-reporting or background-check leads as a category on any page I saw.

- **Top Class Actions** (a publisher that sells campaigns to law firms). Law firms "pay us to run a class action lawsuit investigation." Each campaign is "exclusively owned by you on a month-by-month basis." No prices are listed (read). It runs FCRA employment background-check investigations (snippet, 2025–2026). It says it is "not a law firm, lawyer referral service, or prepaid legal services plan." This is the model to copy: a law firm pays a flat fee per sponsored, firm-named investigation page. https://topclassactions.com/work-with-us/aaj ; https://topclassactions.com/faqs/top-class-actions-get-paid-write-articles/
- **ClassAction.org.** Hosts sponsored investigation pages; the background-check page names Berger Montague as sponsor. Its compensation terms are not stated on the page (read). https://www.classaction.org/employment-background-checks
- **Legal Brand Marketing.** A pay-per-lead legal lead seller, operating since 2004. Its site mentions an affiliate program for website publishers. The practice areas I saw (injury, bankruptcy, criminal, family and so on) do not include consumer or FCRA cases. No payout is published (snippet). https://legalbrandmarketing.com/
- **4LegalLeads.** Pay-per-lead for attorneys with "40+ practice areas" (snippet). I found no publisher program and no consumer or FCRA category. https://4legalleads.com/categories-california
- **Lawyer.com affiliate program (FlexOffers).** "24% of sales," where the sale is a submitted legal lead. The page was updated 2025-01-24 and says FlexOffers is **not currently offering** the program (read). https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/lawyer-com-affiliate-program
- **Credit-repair and debt affiliate programs** (Legal Harbor, The Credit People, Dispute Wizard and others). These pay per sale or per lead for credit repair, not FCRA lawsuits. All figures come from affiliate-blog roundups (third-party, unverified), so I have not repeated them here.

## 3. What the ethics rules allow

**ABA Model Rule 7.2(b)** (text as reproduced at courtrules.net, NH page): "A lawyer shall not give anything of value to a person for recommending the lawyer's services except that a lawyer may (1) pay the reasonable costs of advertisements or communications permitted by this Rule …"
https://www.courtrules.net/new_hampshire/new-hampshire-professional-conduct/rule-7-2 (americanbar.org returned 403)

**ABA Comment [5]** says:
- "a lawyer may pay others for generating client leads, such as Internet-based client leads, as long as the lead generator does not recommend the lawyer."
- The lawyer "must not pay a lead generator that states, implies or creates a reasonable impression that it is recommending the lawyer."
- "A communication contains a recommendation if it endorses or vouches for a lawyer's credentials, abilities, competence, character, or other professional qualities."
- Secondary sources add that the lead generator must also comply with Rule 7.1 (no false or misleading statements) and Rule 5.4 (no sharing of legal fees) (snippet).

**NYSBA Ethics Opinion 1294** (March 11, 2026). A lawyer's payment to a platform is "a permissible lead-generation payment" if three conditions hold:
- the platform does not recommend the lawyer,
- it uses neutral, disclosed selection criteria, and
- the fee does not vary "depending on whether the lead results in retention of the lawyer" (quoting Opinion 1131).

It also says the lawyer may not pay a lead generator that "has analyzed a person's legal problems when determining which lawyer should receive the referral" (read). https://nysba.org/?p=294697

**NYSBA Ethics Opinion 1267** (June 18, 2024). A matching site that promised "well-qualified, vetted lawyers" was making a recommendation. The lawyers' fees to it were improper payments for a recommendation. Calling one lawyer "the right" lawyer after analyzing the person's problem also counts as recommending (read). https://nysba.org/?p=170984

**NYSBA Ethics Opinion 1271** (August 2024). Paying a non-lawyer platform a **percentage of the legal fee** breaks Rule 5.4(a), which bars sharing fees with non-lawyers (snippet). https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1271-sharing-of-legal-fees-with-non-lawyer/

**States are stricter in places:**
- **New Jersey, ACPE Opinion 741 / CAA 47** (2021, pre-2024). Titled "Lawyers Shall Not Pay For Client Referrals; Purported 'Leads' Offered by Marketing Companies May Be Disguised Referrals." I saw snippets only; the full text was blocked. https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/advisory-committee-on-professional-ethics/2021/acp741-1.html
- **Michigan, RI-365** (2013, pre-2024; I did not check whether it is still current). It held that a per-lead fee to a non-lawyer site that does not name the lawyer is payment "for recommending the lawyer's services" (read). https://www.michbar.org/opinions/ethics/numbered_opinions/ri-365
- **Florida, Rule 4-7.22.** A "tips or leads generator" counts as a "qualifying provider" and must comply with the rule, and lawyers must check its compliance. A later amendment requires at least four participating lawyers from different firms (snippet; I did not confirm the current text). https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etad/quick-reference-checklist-print-internet-sponsored-social-media-not-targeted-tv-radio-ads/

### Practical reading for this site

**Generally allowed:**
- A flat or monthly fee for a clearly labeled sponsored placement that names the firm (an attorney ad).
- A fixed per-lead fee that:
  - is the same whether or not the reader hires the firm,
  - is not a share of the fee or settlement, and
  - comes with no "best/vetted/right lawyer for you" language and no case-by-case routing based on the reader's facts.

**Not allowed:**
- A percentage of fees or recoveries.
- Pay only when the reader signs up or a suit is filed (as in the McCarthy CRO model).
- Endorsing or vouching for the firm.
- Choosing a firm for the reader after analyzing their situation.

**Check the state.** The lawyer's state rule governs. New Jersey, Michigan and Florida are stricter or add registration duties. Paid pages also bring TCPA consent and FTC disclosure duties.
